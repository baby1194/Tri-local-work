Forensic findings CASEID

1) Domain context:
A generic institutional records office manages ordinary paperwork through standardized intake forms, unique document identifiers, and versioned retention schedules. New documents are logged upon arrival, assigned sequential identifiers, and routed to designated custodians based on subject matter. Retention periods vary by record class; most operational documents are archived after one fiscal year, while compliance records are preserved for seven years. The records office maintains an audit trail capturing every creation, modification, and access event, including the timestamp, actor identifier, and field-level changes. Access controls restrict document viewing to authorized roles, and periodic audits verify that retention schedules are applied consistently. Any deviation from standard filing procedures, such as off-channel submissions or unversioned edits, is flagged for review. The system relies on structured metadata, including document type, date, and custodian, to ensure traceability and accountability across all recorded transactions.

2) What occurred:
The actor created a series of dated records within a non-standard filing channel, each labeled with generic descriptors for animal preparation, match scheduling, and betting arrangements. A separate document series, identified by a distinct prefix, detailed the handling of injured animals, with fields noting condition assessments and disposition decisions. The actor accessed a third document set containing contact lists and route logs, modifying several fields to obscure geographic details. These alterations were logged in the audit trail with timestamps clustered within a narrow window, indicating batch editing. No approval fields were populated for any of the records, and the documents were never routed to the standard compliance custodian. Instead, they remained in a temporary holding folder associated with the actor's account.

3) Materials/access observed:
The actor utilized a named account with elevated read permissions but standard write restrictions. Access patterns showed repeated retrieval of template forms from an archive repository, followed by immediate modification and storage in a user-specific directory. The documents contained embedded metadata referencing multiple unnamed external channels, with field values formatted to match internal coding conventions. A single spreadsheet artifact, linked to the betting records, included columns for outcome tracking and financial tallies, with formulas referencing cell ranges that no longer exist in the current system schema. The actor's session logs indicated concurrent access to three different record classes, violating standard segregation-of-duties controls.

4) Indicators & artifacts:
Key detection indicators include the use of a non-standard filing channel for operational documents, batch editing patterns within a narrow time window, and the absence of required approval fields. The presence of documents with distinct prefixes in a temporary holding folder, combined with unversioned edits, suggests deliberate circumvention of standard retention procedures. Metadata inconsistencies, such as external channel references within internal documents, and formula references to non-existent schema elements, further support the pattern of off-channel activity. The actor's account access to multiple record classes simultaneously, without corresponding role-based justification, provides an additional audit anomaly for correlation with other system events.
