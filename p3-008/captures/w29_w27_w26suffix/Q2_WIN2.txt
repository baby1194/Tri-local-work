Forensic findings CASEID
1) Domain context: An institutional records office maintains an official registry of student enrollments, course completions, grades, degree conferrals, and diplomas issued. Records are created at enrollment, updated at term completion and graduation, and retained in controlled storage with retention schedules, access logs, and periodic internal audits. Each record carries fields such as student identifier, program, term dates, grade points, conferral date, and issuing authority. Diplomas are serialized and matched against the conferral record. Registrar operations expose verification channels — letters, electronic transcripts, sealed documents, or API lookups — each tied to an authoritative system of record. Auditors compare issued artifacts against source records to detect inconsistencies.

2) What occurred: The actor obtained or constructed a document styled as a university transcript, including fields for program name, course listings, term dates, grades, and cumulative grade points. A second document styled as a diploma was prepared, bearing a conferral date, degree title, serial identifier, and issuing-authority signature line. The actor populated these documents with a named account identifier and a dated record consistent with a completed program of study, despite the absence of an enrollment record in the issuing institution's registry. The actor then directed the document toward a verification channel, including a registrar letter response and a background-verification lookup, with the objective of a match between the presented document and an authoritative record.

3) Materials/access observed: The actor accessed a template or fabricated source for the transcript and diploma documents, including fields for student identifier, program, term dates, course grades, degree title, and serial number. The actor accessed a verification channel used by the employer, including a registrar correspondence channel and a background-verification service channel. No enrollment record, course completion record, or conferral record existed in the issuing institution's system of record corresponding to the identifiers presented.

4) Indicators & artifacts: A transcript document with course, grade, and term fields lacking a corresponding enrollment record in the issuing registry. A diploma document with a serial identifier, conferral date, and signature line not present in the institution's diploma issuance log. A named account identifier appearing in employer-facing documents but absent from the registrar's student registry. A verification request channel (registrar correspondence or background-verification lookup) returning or being positioned to return a match inconsistent with the source records.
