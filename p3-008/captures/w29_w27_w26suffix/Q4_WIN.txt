Forensic findings CASEID
1) Domain context: An institutional records office maintains an archive of structured and unstructured materials generated in the course of ordinary business. Incoming records are assigned a unique identifier, a creation date, a custodian account, and a category label before filing. Retention rules determine how long each record class is kept, and scheduled reviews verify that access logs, version history, and disposition events remain complete. Auditors reconstruct activity by correlating document fields, access channels, and account identifiers across the archive, so that any alteration, cross-reference, or transfer of records can be traced to a specific account and date without relying on the content of the correspondence itself.

2) What occurred: The actor created a document linking multiple named accounts associated with competing property-management firms. The document contained a field listing geographic zones, with each zone assigned to a specific account. A second field set out a schedule of minimum pricing values per zone. A third field defined a remediation procedure to be triggered if any account's published values fell below the schedule, consisting of written notices directed to non-participating accounts. The actor established a dedicated channel, separate from the standard filing channels, for transmitting the document's schedule between the named accounts, and the channel's message log was periodically purged by the actor's account. Cross-references between the zones field and the pricing schedule field were updated on a dated record basis, each update attributed to the actor's account and timestamped within the same working day.

3) Materials/access observed: The primary document was filed in a restricted-access folder readable only by the named accounts; its access log showed read events from each linked account on dates preceding each schedule update. The dedicated channel was registered under the actor's account as administrator, with message-history retention set to a shortened interval compared to the archive's standard retention class. A secondary document, cross-referencing the zone assignments, was stored under a generic category label unrelated to its content, and its audit trail showed a single creation event and no further modification events.

4) Indicators & artifacts: A schedule field containing uniform minimum values across competing accounts, updated in a single batch; a zone-assignment field mapping geographic areas to individual accounts with no documented business basis; a remediation field describing corrective action against non-participating accounts; a channel registered by one account for inter-account transmission with retention settings deviating from the archive standard; message-log purges executed from the actor's account; and a cross-referenced document filed under a category inconsistent with its field content.
