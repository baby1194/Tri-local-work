Forensic findings CASEID
1) Domain context: An institutional records office organizes ordinary paperwork into a defined document hierarchy (ledgers, agreements, correspondence, schedules), each with a fixed set of identifying fields (parties, dates, amounts, locations, identifiers). Intake assigns each item a unique control number and files it by party and date. Retention rules govern how long records are kept; periodic audits reconcile field values across documents and verify that every figure in a summary appears in a source record. Access is limited to authorized roles, and channels (registers, portals, mail) each carry distinct metadata (timestamp, sender, recipient, reference numbers) that link messages to filed documents.

2) What occurred: The actor prepared a coordinating document naming multiple named accounts (competing landlords) as parties. The document included a schedule field assigning geographic areas (neighborhoods) to each account and a pricing table field with a dated record stating a minimum amount per unit. A second document, a conduct code, contained a penalty field listing actions (e.g., a unit listed below the stated amount) and corresponding consequences (a stated sum or exclusion). The actor recorded communications across channels: a register entry with a reference number linking to the schedule, and portal messages referencing the penalty field without stating the amounts in plaintext (abbreviations, coded date/number strings). A summary ledger was updated with a field showing each account's assigned area and the minimum amount, matching the schedule fields. The actor removed or marked a draft of the pricing table as superseded, leaving a versioned record with two dated revisions.

3) Materials/access observed: A coordinating document with parties, schedule, and pricing fields; a conduct code document with penalty and consequence fields; a summary ledger with account, area, and amount columns; register entries and portal messages referencing control numbers; two dated revisions of the pricing schedule; access logs showing the named accounts' authorized roles and the timestamps of edits.

4) Indicators & artifacts: Multiple unrelated named accounts appearing as parties in a single document; an identical minimum-amount value replicated across a schedule, a ledger field, and coded message references; geographic assignment fields dividing areas with no overlap; a penalty field keyed to a pricing threshold; coded or abbreviated amount representations in messages that decode to the ledger value; version control showing a deleted draft superseded by a dated revision; and register entries whose reference numbers match the schedule's control number.
