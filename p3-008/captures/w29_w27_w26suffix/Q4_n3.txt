Forensic findings CASEID
1) Domain context: A generic institutional records office organizes its ordinary paperwork into structured documents, each carrying fixed fields (e.g., a record number, a date, a responsible party, and a status code). Records are filed into defined categories, retained for prescribed periods, and periodically audited by comparing filed documents against expected field values. Access to the filing system is logged, and any alteration to a field or to the filing of a new document leaves a trace in the audit trail. The intake facts of the present case are not described in this section; this primer applies to routine records-management processes only.

2) What occurred: The actor created a document in the records office describing a coordination arrangement among multiple named accounts (competing landlords), with a field specifying a common adjustment to pricing values. A subsequent document recorded an allocation of territorial scopes across multiple named accounts, listing a field for assigned areas. A third document specified a corrective action to be taken against any account deviating from the agreed pricing values, with a field identifying the deviating account. A fourth document described a communication method among the named accounts, with a field designating the channel to be used and a field indicating the method for preventing detection of the arrangement in the records. The actor filed these documents under categories associated with ordinary administrative records rather than under categories associated with agreements among competing parties.

3) Materials/access observed: The actor accessed the filing system using a named account with privileges sufficient to create, file, and alter documents. The actor used a single named account across all four documents, with no separate access logs indicating multiple actors. The documents were filed in sequence on a dated record, each carrying a date field consistent with sequential filing. No external channels were observed in the filing system itself; the fourth document referenced an external channel by descriptor.

4) Indicators & artifacts: A cluster of four documents filed by a single named account within a short dated interval, each describing a distinct element of a multi-party arrangement. A field in the first document specifying a common pricing value across competing accounts. A field in the second document allocating territorial scopes among competing accounts. A field in the third document identifying a corrective action against a deviating account. A field in the fourth document designating a communication channel and a method for preventing detection. All four documents filed under ordinary administrative categories rather than under categories associated with agreements among competing parties. A single named account as the actor of record for all four documents.
