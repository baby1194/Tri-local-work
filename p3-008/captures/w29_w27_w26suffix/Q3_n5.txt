Forensic findings CASEID
1) Domain context:
An institutional records office maintains a centralized, append-only repository for all formal correspondence, transaction logs, and approval documents. Incoming submissions are indexed by date, author identifier, and subject classification, then routed to the responsible custodian via a secure internal channel. Each document carries a unique control number, a version stamp, and an access-control field that enumerates permitted readers. Retention is governed by policy: original filings are preserved for the statutory period, with immutable audit trails recording every read, copy, and transmission event. Periodic audits compare access logs against duty rosters to confirm that only authorized personnel viewed each record; any deviation between an actor's role and the sensitivity tier of a document is flagged for manual review.

2) What occurred:
The actor received an intake document via a messaging channel. The document contained a field describing a scheduled corporate disclosure with a date marker set for the following week. A second field in the same document enumerated three requested actions: a selection parameter, a timing parameter, and a routing parameter. The actor opened a draft document referencing a named securities account and populated three fields: one mapping the selection parameter to a specific instrument identifier, one mapping the timing parameter to a pre-announcement window, and one mapping the routing parameter to a sequence of accounts intended to interpose intermediaries between the original account and the final execution venue. The actor transmitted the draft through the same messaging channel, attaching a reference to a compliance-review queue that the actor did not hold permission to access.

3) Materials/access observed:
- One intake document containing a dated disclosure record and three action fields.
- One draft document referencing a named account, with three populated parameter fields (instrument identifier, execution window, routing sequence).
- One messaging channel used for both intake and transmission.
- One reference to a compliance-review queue; the actor's access record showed no entitlement to that queue.
- One named securities account, appearing in the draft's account field.

4) Indicators & artifacts:
- Presence of a dated future-disclosure field in a non-filing document.
- A draft document populating timing fields earlier than the actor's role tier would normally permit.
- A routing-sequence field enumerating more than one intermediate account.
- Access-log entries showing the actor opened the compliance-review queue reference without a corresponding entitlement record.
- Transmission of the draft through the intake channel rather than a formal filing channel.
