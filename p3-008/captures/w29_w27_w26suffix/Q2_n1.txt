Forensic findings CASEID
1) Domain context:
Institutional records offices function as custodial repositories for academic credentials and employment verification data. Standard practice involves centralized filing systems where transcripts, diplomas, and registrar records are maintained in both physical and digital formats. Documents are cataloged using unique identifiers, issue dates, and issuing institution details. Retention policies typically require preservation for the duration of employment plus a statutory period. Audit procedures include periodic reconciliation against source registries, verification of serial numbers, and confirmation of issue dates against institutional calendars. Access controls restrict modification to authorized registrar personnel only, with all changes logged in tamper-evident ledgers. Verification workflows often require direct confirmation through official channels rather than acceptance of document scans alone, creating multiple independent points of failure detection.

2) What occurred:
The actor prepared a document bearing the format and layout of an academic transcript, populating fields for institution name, student identifier, course listings, grade entries, and completion date. A second document was produced in diploma format containing degree classification, conferral date, and institutional seal placement. The actor identified the verification channels through which the named employer's registrar would confirm credential authenticity, including direct registrar inquiry lines and digital verification portals. The actor structured the fabricated records with plausible date sequences and institutional formatting to align with expected verification responses. No actual enrollment records, course completion documentation, or legitimate degree conferral existed in the source registry.

3) Materials/access observed:
The documents referenced a named institution not attended by the actor, a named account with no enrollment history, and dated records falling outside any period of legitimate institutional registration. The transcript contained course numbers, credit values, and grade entries with no corresponding entries in the issuing institution's student information system. The diploma included a seal image sourced from public templates rather than an official institutional engraving. The verification channels targeted were the named employer's human resources department and the issuing institution's registrar office.

4) Indicators & artifacts:
- Document serial numbers absent or non-conforming to institutional numbering schemes
- Issue dates inconsistent with institutional academic calendars
- Student identifier not present in source registry upon direct inquiry
- Seal imagery with pixel-level inconsistencies and missing micrographic security features
- Verification attempts through unofficial channels (email addresses, unlisted phone numbers) rather than official registrar endpoints
- Response times and phrasing inconsistent with actual registrar staff communication patterns
- File metadata showing recent creation dates with no institutional processing history
