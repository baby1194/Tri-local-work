Forensic findings CASEID
1) Domain context:
The records office of the institution maintained a formal filing system for standard administrative paperwork. Incoming documents were logged upon receipt, assigned sequential reference numbers, and routed to the appropriate custodial unit based on subject matter. Each file was retained according to a fixed retention schedule, with periodic audits conducted to verify chain-of-custody integrity and data accuracy. Access was restricted to authorized personnel, and all modifications to records required documented justification and secondary approval. Electronic systems generated immutable audit trails capturing user identifiers, timestamps, and IP addresses for every read, write, or delete operation. Paper originals were stored in locked archives, with access logs signed by the archivist on each occasion. Cross-departmental transfers followed a defined chain, with acknowledgment receipts filed at each handoff point. This standard operating environment established the baseline against which anomalous document creation, unauthorized field modifications, and atypical routing patterns were later identified.

2) What occurred:
The actor initiated a series of document preparations involving donor candidate profiles, each containing a named account identifier, a date of birth field, a debt obligation amount field, and a consent attestation field. The actor populated the consent attestation fields with pre-executed signatures not corresponding to the named account holders, and altered the debt obligation amount fields to reflect inflated figures. The actor prepared a routing document listing foreign recipient identifiers, a clinic destination field, and an estimated procedure date field. The actor transmitted the routing document via an encrypted channel to a second account, which was not registered in the institution's official directory. The actor created a secondary medical records file containing an altered health status field and a backdated examination date, and filed it under a different reference number than the original. The actor accessed the retention schedule document and modified the destruction date field for three files, extending the destruction window by one year. Each modification was logged with a user identifier that did not match the authorized custodial role for the affected document class.

3) Materials/access observed:
The actor operated from a named account with a standard employee role, which did not include permission to execute consent attestation documents or modify retention schedule parameters. The actor used a template document for donor profiles that contained a signature field, a debt amount field, and a date field. The actor accessed a directory service to enumerate account identifiers for foreign recipients, and extracted clinic identifier fields from a scheduling database. The actor utilized an encrypted messaging channel outside the institution's approved communication protocols, identified by a channel identifier not present in the institutional network inventory. The actor accessed a locked archive terminal during an off-duty time window, recorded in the access log with a timestamp outside the scheduled audit period.

4) Indicators & artifacts:
Anomalous indicators included: a consent attestation document with a signature field populated without a corresponding biometric or identity verification record in the linked file; a debt obligation amount field value exceeding the institutional threshold by a factor of three; a routing document transmitted to a directory-unregistered account via an unlisted channel identifier; a medical records file with a backdated examination date field that predated the donor candidate's first visit by a date interval; a retention schedule document with a destruction date field modified to an off-cycle value; and an access log entry showing archive terminal usage during a time window with no corresponding duty assignment record. Artifacts included: three donor profile documents with mismatched signature and identity verification fields; one routing document with an unregistered recipient account identifier; one modified retention schedule entry; and one encrypted channel transmission log with a message count of two and a total payload size consistent with document transfer.
