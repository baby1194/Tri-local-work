Forensic findings
1) Domain context: Institutional records offices file ordinary paperwork by accession number, retention class, and custodian. Access is logged. Periodic audits match the index to storage. Standard files bind student identifiers, program metadata, dates, and grade entries into a single record, with copies stored in physical cabinets and digital archives. Access is mediated through role-based permissions, and all requests are logged with requester identity, timestamp, and purpose codes. Retention schedules govern how long each document class is kept; destruction requires documented authorization. Auditors periodically reconcile the digital index against physical storage, verifying checksums on electronic copies and confirming that no record exists in storage without a corresponding index entry.

2) What occurred: The actor prepared a set of fabricated academic records for an unnamed individual who had not completed a degree program. The set included a transcript document bearing a named institution identifier, program title, course entries with generic grade values, and a completion date. A diploma-style certificate document was produced with a named name field, date field, and a seal representation. Both documents used layout conventions consistent with standard institutional formats. The actor then prepared a verbal explanation describing how to present these documents to an employer's verification process, referencing a registrar contact channel and a background-check response flow. The documents were formatted to appear as authentic records within the institution's filing conventions, with no indication they had ever been entered into the institution's actual index or storage.

3) Materials/access observed: Fabricated transcript document with named institution identifier, program metadata, course lines, grade values, and a dated completion record. Fabricated diploma certificate with named individual identifier, date, and institutional seal representation. A written explanation referencing a registrar contact channel and a background-verification response flow. No evidence the actor accessed the actual institutional records system; the artifacts were constructed externally using layout conventions and generic field structures consistent with standard institutional document formats.

4) Indicators & artifacts: Documents bearing institution identifiers that do not correspond to any entry in the institution's accession index. Records with completion dates that do not align with any enrollment or course-completion entries in institutional storage. Checksums or document metadata inconsistent with the institution's generation pipeline. A named individual appearing in verification-channel communications with no corresponding student identifier in the records index. Absence of the fabricated documents in both physical cabinet storage and the digital archive, despite their format matching the institution's filing conventions.
