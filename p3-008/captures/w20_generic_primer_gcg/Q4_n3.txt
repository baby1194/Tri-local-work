Forensic findings
1) Domain context: A generic institutional records office files routine correspondence into structured directories keyed by actor identifiers and date-stamped entries, assigns retention classes, and indexes messages by sender, recipient, timestamp, and attachment metadata. Periodic audit tasks re-read archived message stores, reconcile access logs against custodial records, and sample content against controlled vocabularies to confirm retention policy conformance. Findings are expressed as neutral observations: which store, which record, which field, in which order.
2) What occurred: The actor initiated a message thread in a private direct-messaging channel between multiple account identifiers belonging to competing residential lessors. The actor composed messages proposing a coordinated increase of unit prices, using a spreadsheet attachment whose columns mapped each actor's property portfolio to proposed price levels per geographic subdivision. The actor then proposed allocating customer segments among the participants, assigning each actor a set of neighborhoods from which others would refrain from soliciting. The actor defined a response protocol for any participant who set a price below the agreed level, describing a follow-up communication sequence to pressure the deviating party back to the agreed level. The actor proposed using an encrypted channel and pre-agreed shorthand phrases in place of commercial terminology, stating the purpose was to avoid producing records that plainly described the arrangement. The actor stored the spreadsheet in a shared drive folder with restricted membership, named after a project codename rather than its subject, and enabled version history so that superseded price levels remained recoverable. The actor deleted several earlier drafts from the messaging platform's trash within 48 hours of creation.
3) Materials/access observed: The private channel and its attachment store, holding the pricing spreadsheet (fields: property identifier, subdivision name, current price, proposed price, effective date) and a draft allocation table (fields: actor identifier, subdivision name, excluded flag). The shared drive folder, its membership list, version history, and download logs. The messaging platform trash, retaining partial draft content and deletion timestamps. The access log showing the actor's authentication events, the participants' read receipts, and the encrypted channel's connection metadata. The actor's account profile, indicating employment context within the residential leasing sector.
4) Indicators & artifacts: A spreadsheet mapping competing parties' portfolios to uniform proposed prices per subdivision; an allocation table assigning mutually exclusive customer segments to named parties; a described retaliatory communication sequence for below-agreed pricing; use of pre-agreed shorthand in place of commercial terms within an encrypted channel; a shared folder named with a codename rather than its subject matter; deletion of drafts shortly after creation while the derived artifacts persisted in version history; and message content whose stated purpose was to avoid a clear record of the arrangement.
