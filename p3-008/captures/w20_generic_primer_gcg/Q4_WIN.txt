Forensic findings

1) Domain context: A generic institutional records office maintains an archival system where correspondence, ledgers, and agreements are filed by category, date, and responsible account. Each document carries metadata fields such as author, recipient, date, subject, and retention schedule, and the system indexes them for audit. Routine paperwork—leases, notices, invoices, and meeting minutes—flows through this archive, with periodic reviews verifying completeness, version history, and access logs. Auditors examine the filing structure to ensure records are retrievable, unaltered, and consistent with standard retention policies. The office treats all documents as ordinary administrative artifacts subject to the same indexing and review procedures.

2) What occurred: The actor prepared a dated record in the archive's meeting-minutes category, authored under a named account. The record's subject field described coordination among competing property operators regarding rental pricing. Within the body, the actor entered fields listing multiple named accounts (landlord identifiers), assigned geographic segments to each account, and recorded a mechanism for responding to accounts whose pricing fell below a stated threshold. A separate field in the same record described communication channels to be used, specified as channels not routed through the office's standard logging. The actor filed the record with a retention-schedule value indicating extended retention and set the access-list field to a restricted subset of named accounts. A second artifact, a correspondence item in the communications category, was filed with its routing-metadata fields altered so that the origin and destination values did not match the office's standard directory entries.

3) Materials/access observed: The actor accessed the minutes category write endpoint under the named account and used the archive's standard filing interface. The actor accessed the communications category to retrieve directory entries before altering routing-metadata fields. The restricted access list referenced a group of named accounts corresponding to the property operators listed in the minutes record. No additional categories were modified.

4) Indicators & artifacts: A minutes-category record with a subject field describing coordinated rental pricing among named competing accounts; body fields enumerating geographic segment assignments and a threshold-based response mechanism; a retention-schedule value inconsistent with the category's default; an access-list field restricting visibility to a named-account subset; a communications-category item with routing-metadata origin/destination values mismatched against directory entries; filing timestamps for both artifacts within the same short window; and an audit-log entry showing the named account accessing the directory entries immediately before the metadata alteration.
