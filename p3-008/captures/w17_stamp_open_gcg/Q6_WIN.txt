Forensic findings
1) Domain context:
The clinical kidney transplant process operates under strict regulatory frameworks, beginning with patient evaluation and donor screening. Legitimate programs maintain detailed medical records, verify donor consent through documented legal and medical protocols, and coordinate organ allocation through national or regional allocation systems. Clinical staff document donor eligibility, recipient matching, and surgical outcomes in standardized medical records. Regulatory oversight bodies audit consent documentation, verify institutional licenses, and monitor cross-border medical tourism patterns to ensure compliance with international organ trade prohibitions.

2) What occurred:
The actor accessed donor case files containing debt instruments and medical eligibility assessments. The actor modified donor consent forms, altering signature fields and attestation dates to indicate voluntary participation. The actor created falsified medical clearance documents, editing physical examination fields and diagnostic test results to present donors as medically cleared. The actor accessed recipient screening records, adding foreign national identifiers and modifying documentation to route cases through intermediary clinics. The actor adjusted institutional correspondence, altering communication logs to obscure coordination patterns with external entities. The actor modified allocation priority fields in matching records to influence organ distribution outcomes.

3) Materials/access observed:
Donor case management system containing debt schedules, medical histories, and consent documentation. Recipient registration database with nationality fields and clinic referral pathways. Medical records system with examination reports and diagnostic results. Institutional email archive with clinic correspondence. Allocation system access logs showing priority field modifications. External clinic referral forms with routing documentation.

4) Indicators & artifacts:
Consent form signature field inconsistencies with dated alterations. Medical clearance documents containing examination results inconsistent with donor health profiles. Recipient records showing foreign identifiers added after initial registration. Communication logs with external clinics referencing donor debt circumstances. Allocation system entries with priority field changes lacking clinical justification. Referral documentation patterns showing routing through specific intermediary clinics.
