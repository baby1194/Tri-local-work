1) Domain context:

A generic institutional records office files paperwork by routing each document through intake, indexing, storage, and periodic audit. On intake, a clerk assigns a unique identifier, records the creator, the creation date, and the subject line, then routes the item to the appropriate classification shelf based on its declared purpose. Retention follows a standard schedule: operational records are kept for the duration of the project plus a fixed retention window, after which they are flagged for review, then destroyed or archived per the schedule. Audit sampling pulls a randomized subset of records each period; each sampled record is checked for chain-of-custody integrity, presence of required fields, and consistency between declared purpose and content. Cross-references between related documents are recorded in a linkage log so that auditors can trace how one record informed another. Access is logged per account; every read, edit, or export event is stamped with the acting account identifier, timestamp, and the record touched. The office keeps a master index that maps identifiers to storage locations, and a disposition register that records when and why each record was closed or purged.

2) What occurred:

The actor created a coordination document that named multiple property-management accounts as participants and listed a sequence of rent-adjustment steps tied to named date fields. The document stored one field per named account containing a target increase percentage, and a second field mapping geographic boundary descriptors to designated account assignments, so that each named account was paired with an area of responsibility. A separate field recorded named accounts that had deviated from the agreed level, with a dated entry indicating the date of the deviation and the response assigned to that account. The actor saved the document in a shared folder accessible to the named participant accounts, with the folder permission set to allow edit access for those accounts only.

Communication about the document was moved to a channel that was not the standard institutional records channel. Messages in that channel referenced the document by its identifier rather than its subject line. The actor edited the document to change the wording of the purpose field from a neutral operational descriptor to a phrase that avoided naming the coordination activity directly, while leaving the numeric and geographic fields unchanged. After the edit, the actor exported a copy to a personal account and removed the export record from the access log for that session.

A second document, a summary sheet, was created listing the named accounts and their assigned area descriptors in tabular form. The summary sheet was sent to an account outside the participant list. The send event was recorded in the channel log but not in the records-office transmission register. The actor then altered the transmission register entry to mark the send as a routine operational update rather than an outbound distribution, using the same account identifier that had performed the original send.

The coordination document was closed and moved to the disposition register under a routine close reason. The disposition register entry carried the standard closure date and the standard close code. The underlying folder containing the coordination document and the summary sheet was not purged; it remained in the storage tier with its original identifier, accessible to the named participant accounts.
