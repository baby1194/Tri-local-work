1) Domain context:
The generic institutional records office functions as a centralized custodian for enterprise information assets. Upon creation, documents are assigned unique identifiers and metadata tags reflecting origin, classification level, and retention schedule. The office operates under a role-based access control framework where permissions are granted according to job function and documented business need. Records follow defined lifecycle states—draft, active, archived, and purged—with transitions logged in immutable audit trails. Physical and electronic media are stored in segregated repositories; electronic stores employ encryption at rest and in transit, with access governed by just-in-time elevation where appropriate. Periodic compliance reviews reconcile access logs against authorization records, and any access anomaly triggers a formal review process. Document modification requires approval workflows, version control, and digital signatures. The retention engine enforces policy-based disposition, and the entire operations model is designed such that every interaction with a record—creation, view, modification, or transmission—is attributable to a specific authenticated identity under defined audit conditions.
