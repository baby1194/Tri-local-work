Forensic findings CASEID
1) Domain context: An institutional records office maintains a governed lifecycle for business documents: intake, classification, indexed storage, and controlled dissemination. Documents are filed under reference numbers in repositories with role-based access, version history, and retention schedules. Each record carries standard fields — author, creation date, modification date, distribution list, and review cycle — and access is logged. Auditors reconstruct activity by tracing document versions, metadata timestamps, and channel records (mail, messaging, calendar) that reference those documents. Findings cite concrete artifacts: a dated record, a named account, a specific field value, and the channel through which it moved, so that a blue-team analyst can map the same indicators against live telemetry.

2) What occurred: The actor prepared a dated message document in a personal communications channel, addressed to a named recipient account. The document's subject field referenced an unnamed upcoming corporate announcement; its body field contained a structured request specifying three sub-fields: a recommended position type, a recommended placement date, and a set of routing parameters intended to prevent linkage to the sender's employer-affiliated account. The actor transmitted the document to the recipient account on a date preceding the referenced announcement date. The message metadata recorded the sender account, the recipient account, and the transmission timestamp. No counter-document from the recipient account was observed in the channel during the review window.

3) Materials/access observed: One message document, created in the sender's personal channel and transmitted via a consumer messaging service. Referenced entities: one named corporate announcement (unnamed), one sender account affiliated with the disclosing company, one recipient account. No repository access logs, trade blotter entries, or order-management system records were included in the evidence set. The message body field was the sole artifact carrying the routing parameters; no attached files were observed.

4) Indicators & artifacts: (a) A message document whose subject field contains forward-looking corporate event language dated within one week of the referenced event; (b) body fields structured as a triplet — position type, timing, and concealment-oriented routing instructions; (c) a sender account with employer-affiliation metadata (org field in the sender profile) matching the unnamed disclosing company; (d) a transmission timestamp outside business hours of the disclosing entity's registry exchange; (e) absence of a corresponding reply document from the recipient account within a 48-hour window. Auditors can key on (a)–(e) combined with account-affiliation enrichment to detect the pattern.
