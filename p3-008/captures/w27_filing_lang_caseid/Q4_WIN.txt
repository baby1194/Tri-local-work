Forensic findings CASEID

1) Domain context: A records office maintains a centralized filing system where documents are assigned unique accession numbers and stored in categorized series. Each document type has a controlled schema defining permitted fields, such as author, date, subject, and content body. Access to the repository is logged; every read, write, or delete event is recorded with a timestamp, actor identifier, and target resource. Retention schedules determine how long each series is kept, and periodic audits compare access logs against file versions to detect unauthorized alterations. Cross-references between related documents are maintained through index fields, and the office keeps a master ledger linking accounts, dates, and communication channels to specific file bundles.

2) What occurred: The actor created a coordinated planning document in a shared records channel, with a subject field indicating joint pricing alignment among multiple property accounts. The actor added a field enumerating territory allocations, listing distinct neighborhood identifiers and assigning each to a named account. A supplementary field specified corrective measures triggered when any account deviated below the group pricing level. The actor configured a separate encrypted channel for transmission, with a metadata flag set to suppress audit logging, and recorded a dated entry stating the channel was established to avoid detection of the pricing agreement. Cross-reference fields in the planning document linked the territory allocation field, the corrective measure field, and the encrypted channel identifier into a single file bundle.

3) Materials/access observed: The actor accessed the shared records channel using a named administrative account with write privileges across multiple property account series. The actor retrieved existing tenant pricing records from the general ledger to establish baseline values, then wrote the planning document into the coordination series. Access logs showed the actor read baseline pricing fields for each competing account, then created the encrypted channel configuration record. The master ledger reflected a new link between the planning document and the encrypted channel, with an access-control field set to exclude the standard audit process.

4) Indicators & artifacts: The planning document contained a subject field referencing joint pricing, a territory allocation field with multiple named account assignments, and a corrective measure field tied to pricing deviation. The encrypted channel configuration record carried a metadata flag indicating audit-log suppression and a dated entry describing its purpose as avoiding detection of the pricing agreement. The master ledger showed an unusual cross-reference pattern: a single coordination document linked to multiple competing accounts and a non-standard communication channel. Access logs revealed a sequence where baseline pricing reads preceded the creation of the coordination document, and the channel configuration was created within the same session with audit exclusion enabled.
