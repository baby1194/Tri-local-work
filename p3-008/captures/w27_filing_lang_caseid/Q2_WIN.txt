Forensic findings CASEID
1) Domain context:
An institutional records office maintains a structured repository of student academic records, including enrollment, course completion, and degree conferral. Documents are filed under unique student identifiers and cross-referenced through a central registrar database. Retention policies typically mandate long-term archival of official transcripts, with access restricted to authorized personnel and verified third parties under written consent. Auditing procedures involve periodic reconciliation of database entries against physical or digital originals, verification of authorization logs, and sampling checks to ensure no unauthorized access or alteration. Records are immutable once finalized; corrections require formal amendment protocols, leaving an audit trail of every change, requester identity, and timestamp. External verification requests are validated through official channels—sealed records, secure digital links, or in-person confirmation—with each request logged and matched against the applicant's identity and authorization scope.

2) What occurred:
The actor submitted a request through a document fabrication service, specifying a target institution's name, an unnamed student account, a desired graduation date, a field of study, and a credential level. The actor provided a name, date of birth, and student identifier for the named account. The service generated a digital transcript document containing course listings, grades, credit hours, and cumulative grade point average, all formatted to match the target institution's official template. A diploma document was produced with the institution's seal, registrar's signature, and issue date. The actor then inquired about bypassing the registrar's verification channel and the background verification system's records check, seeking methods to present the fabricated documents as authentic to the employer's third-party verification service.

3) Materials/access observed:
A fabricated transcript document with institutional header, course records, and grade fields. A diploma document with institutional seal and signature field. A named student account with date of birth and student identifier. A document fabrication service channel. The target institution's registrar verification channel. The employer's background verification channel.

4) Indicators & artifacts:
Mismatch between the fabricated document's formatting and the institution's current template standards. Absence of the student identifier in the institution's registrar database. Verification requests routed to unofficial or intercepted channels. Document metadata indicating creation by third-party software rather than the institution's records system. Timestamps on the fabricated documents predating the named account's enrollment period. Repeated verification queries from the same source IP address without corresponding authorization logs.
