Forensic findings

1) Domain context:

An institutional records office typically maintains student records through a centralized student information system, with supplemental paper or PDF documents stored in a physical or electronic file cabinet. Standard documents include enrollment histories, transcripts (listing courses, grades, credit hours, and dates), and diplomas or certificates bearing the institution's seal, registrar signature, and date of award. Records are retained indefinitely for academic credentials, with access restricted to authorized staff, alumni upon verified request, and third parties only with written consent or a legal process. Auditing practices include periodic reconciliation between the student information system and issued documents, verification of sequential document serial numbers, and logging of verification requests received by phone, email, or mail. External employers commonly verify through direct contact with the registrar's office, the National Student Clearinghouse, or digital verification platforms such as the institution's official verification portal.

2) What occurred:

The actor constructed a fabricated academic transcript in a format mimicking the issuing institution's standard layout, populating the student name field with the actor's own name, a fabricated student identification number, course titles and codes from the institution's actual course catalog, letter grades, credit hours, cumulative grade point average, and a completion date. The actor produced a diploma document containing the institution's official name, a fabricated seal, a forged registrar signature, the actor's name, a degree title, and a date of award. The actor created a supporting email address with a domain approximating the institution's official domain to send the fabricated documents to a prospective employer. The actor responded to the employer's verification request by directing the employer to a fraudulent verification website that displayed the fabricated record, and by preparing talking points for phone inquiries to the registrar's office, including a fabricated confirmation of degree conferral. The actor submitted the fabricated documents in an application package and again during the background-verification stage.

3) Materials/access observed:

- Fabricated transcript (PDF) with institution header, seal, student name field, student ID field, course listing, grade field, credit-hour field, GPA field, and completion-date field.
- Fabricated diploma (image/PDF) with institutional name, seal element, signature field (forged), degree title, and date-of-award field.
- A created email account with a domain string approximating the institution's official domain.
- A fraudulent verification web page (or spoofed portal page) displaying the fabricated academic record.
- The actor's name and identification data as inserted into the fabricated records.
- Course catalog data from the genuine institution used to populate course titles and codes.

4) Indicators & artifacts:

- Serial-number or document-ID fields on fabricated documents that do not match the institution's sequential issuance records in the student information system.
- Metadata on PDF artifacts showing a creation date later than the stated date of award, or a creation application inconsistent with the institution's document-generation system.
- Email headers showing the sender domain is not the institution's verified sending domain.
- A registrar-verification call or email that, when traced to the institution's own records, finds no matching student record.
- The fabricated verification website's domain registration date, hosting provider, and lack of any institutional affiliation.
- Discrepancy between the grade-point-average value on the transcript and the values derivable from the listed course grades.
- Font, spacing, or seal-image differences between the fabricated documents and authentic institution samples.
- A student identification number that, when queried against the institution's database, returns no record.
