1) Domain context: Institutional records offices maintain student and alumni documents within controlled document management systems. Each academic record typically includes a unique student identifier, enrollment dates, degree conferral dates, and grade point averages, which are stored in immutable audit trails. Physical and digital copies of transcripts and diplomas are generated under strict chain-of-custody protocols, with watermarks, security paper, and QR codes linking to a central registry. Access to the registry is role-based, restricted to authorized registrars, and all modification attempts are logged with timestamps and user identifiers. Background verification channels—credentialing portals, direct registrar calls, and third-party verification services—cross-reference the claimed document metadata against the registry. Records offices conduct periodic reconciliation audits comparing issued documents against the master ledger, flagging any record that cannot be validated via the registry key or security features.

2) What occurred: The actor first identified the employer's verification pathway, noting whether the background screen used a direct registrar lookup or a third-party credentialing vendor. The actor then produced a synthetic transcript record and a corresponding diploma artifact. The transcript carried a plausible student identifier, a named account entry with the actor's identifying fields substituted, attendance dates consistent with the claimed graduation year, and a course listing with grade values aligned to the target degree. The diploma field set included an issuing institution name, degree title, conferral date, and a seal graphic. The actor prepared two variants of the transcript (PDF and scanned image) and one diploma artifact, all bearing consistent registry-style reference numbers. The actor then transmitted the documents through a personal email channel and a cloud file-share link to the recruiter, and, upon a verification request, supplied the registry reference number in a written response to the background check questionnaire.

3) Materials/access observed: The actor observed the employer's onboarding questionnaire (fields for highest education, institution, year, credential ID). The actor accessed publicly available templates of the target institution's transcript layout, obtained the institution's official seal graphic from the public web, and generated the synthetic documents using common office-suite and image-editing tools. No access to the institution's internal registry was obtained; the actor relied on publicly visible metadata only.

4) Indicators & artifacts: A transcript and diploma lacking a valid registry reference number that returns "not found" on the institution's credential-verification portal. Mismatched fonts or color profiles between the seal and the body text. File metadata showing recent creation timestamps inconsistent with the claimed graduation year. The background-verification channel returning a "record not verified" status. A cloud file-share link in the applicant's correspondence containing the two documents. The written response to the questionnaire containing a registry reference number that fails lookup.
