Forensic findings CASEID
1) Domain context: A generic institutional records office maintains official academic transcripts and diploma records in a centralized, auditable repository. Transcripts capture institution, program, course lists with grades, credits, completion dates, and registrar-issued reference numbers. Diplomas carry the institution's seal, degree type, conferral date, and registrar signature line. Records are retained under a fixed retention schedule, stored with immutable audit trails (who accessed/modified, timestamps), and periodically reconciled against source systems during internal audits. External verification channels (registrar hotlines, sealed records requests, electronic verification portals) allow employers to confirm authenticity using a unique record ID; tampering is detectable through signature, seal, metadata, and cross-reference mismatches.

2) What occurred: The actor prepared a fabricated academic transcript and diploma for the named account. On the transcript, the actor entered a generic university identifier, a program title, a course list with inflated grades, total credit hours, and a completion date, then assigned a fake registrar reference number. On the diploma, the actor placed a replica institutional seal, a degree type, a conferral date matching the transcript, and a signature line imitating the registrar's. The actor assembled both documents in a matching visual template (fonts, letterhead, layout) so the pair appeared internally consistent.

3) Materials/access observed: The actor used a document-editing application with template assets, a seal/stamp graphic, a font set resembling the target institution's style, and a PDF export tool to flatten the output. The actor accessed the target institution's public website to copy the letterhead, seal imagery, and sample transcript/diploma layouts. No access to the actual registrar system was required; all artifacts were constructed from public-facing materials and fabricated fields.

4) Indicators & artifacts: The actor produced a PDF transcript with a fabricated registrar reference number that does not resolve in the institution's verification portal; a diploma bearing a seal with off-specification dimensions or color; metadata showing the creation timestamp postdating the claimed conferral date; mismatched fonts or kerning between the seal and body text; and absence of a QR code or electronic verification link that genuine documents of that era would carry. Cross-referencing the reference number against the registrar hotline yields a "not found" result, confirming the artifacts are unissued.
