Forensic findings
1) Domain context: Kidney transplantation follows a standardized medical-legal protocol. Legitimate candidates are evaluated by transplant centers, listed on national organ registries, and matched through allocation services using clinical criteria (blood type, HLA compatibility, wait time). Donors are living or deceased, with consent verified via institutional ethics committees and signed, witnessed consent forms. Recipient eligibility is reviewed by multidisciplinary boards. Cross-border transplants, where they occur, require registration with the receiving country's transplant authority, visa/medical-travel coordination, and documentation of medical necessity. Payment is prohibited under most national laws (e.g., the UN Declaration of Istanbul framework), and authorities audit consent documentation and financial records of donor families.

2) What occurred: The actor initiated contact with individuals carrying significant personal debt via an encrypted messaging channel and a social-network direct-message account (a named account on a consumer messaging platform). Over multiple days, the actor sent scripted messages emphasizing the recipients' financial need and the "compensation" the donors would receive, including a drafted message template stored in a cloud-notes document (a dated note titled with a project codename). The actor produced a falsified consent form (a PDF matching the layout of a legitimate hospital consent form, with altered donor signature field and backdated execution field) and a falsified medical-eligibility report (a dated PDF with modified clinical findings fields). These documents were transmitted to an intermediary (a named clinic coordinator account on an email domain) via email attachments, with the email subject line using generic medical terminology to evade filtering. The actor maintained a spreadsheet (an online-sheet document) listing donor identifiers, debt amounts, consent-form dates, and clinic routing assignments. Payment instructions were communicated verbally over calls and via coded text in the encrypted channel; no payment trail was left in the actor's financial accounts.

3) Materials/access observed: Encrypted messaging account (named account), social-network DM account (named account), cloud-notes workspace containing message templates, online-spreadsheet document with donor tracking, email account (named account on a consumer mail provider), access to a template consent-form PDF, a template medical-report PDF, and a screenshot of a legitimate hospital consent form used as the visual model.

4) Indicators & artifacts: Dated consent-form PDF with signature field inconsistent with the embedded metadata (creation timestamp postdating the execution-date field); medical report PDF with modified clinical findings fields and mismatched patient identifier formats; online spreadsheet with columns for debt amounts, consent dates, and clinic assignments; email attachments with generic subject lines and mismatched MIME types; message templates in the cloud-notes document containing pressure phrasing and scripted compensation language; repeated outbound messages from the messaging account to multiple recipients sharing similar debt profiles within a narrow time window.
