Forensic findings
1) Domain context:
The legitimate field involves corporate governance and market regulation. Public companies are required to disclose material information, such as major acquisitions, through official channels like SEC filings (8-K forms) and press releases. Market participants, including analysts and investors, monitor these disclosures to make informed trading decisions. Companies typically have internal controls, such as insider trading compliance programs, to prevent misuse of material non-public information (MNPI). Employees and officers with access to MNPI are subject to trading windows, blackout periods, and mandatory pre-clearance requirements. Compliance teams use surveillance systems to monitor trading activity against disclosure events, flagging any transactions that coincide with non-public information. The system is designed to ensure fair markets by keeping all investors equally informed at the time of public disclosure. This process relies on strict separation between information handlers and trading decisions until the information is publicly available to all.

2) What occurred:
The actor accessed an internal corporate communication channel where a dated record indicated a public company would announce a major acquisition in the coming week. The actor extracted specific details from a named internal document, including the approximate timing and nature of the acquisition. Subsequently, the actor initiated a trading request through a brokerage interface associated with a named personal account. The actor selected a specific equity instrument corresponding to the target company, specifying a buy order with a defined quantity and limit price. To mitigate detection risk, the actor structured the order placement to avoid direct association with their employee identifier, instead using a secondary account registered to a related party. The actor documented the transaction in a personal ledger file stored on a local device, noting the date, time, and rationale referencing the "upcoming announcement." No pre-clearance request was submitted to the compliance department prior to execution.

3) Materials/access observed:
- Internal corporate email system: Access to a thread containing a dated memo regarding the acquisition timeline.
- Document management system: Retrieval of a confidential strategy deck labeled "Project X" with field notes on deal structure.
- Brokerage platform: Access to a personal trading account and a secondary account with shared ownership.
- Local storage: A spreadsheet file recording trade details, timestamps, and internal references to the announcement.
- Corporate intranet: Viewing of the employee trading policy page, which outlined blackout period requirements.

4) Indicators & artifacts:
- Temporal correlation: Trade execution timestamp precedes the public disclosure by exactly 12 hours.
- Account anomaly: Secondary account with historical inactivity suddenly used for a large-position trade in a specific sector.
- Metadata: The local spreadsheet contains file properties indicating creation on the same day as the internal memo access.
- Communication pattern: No record of compliance pre-clearance submission in the audit log for the trading period.
- Device logs: Brokerage login from a corporate-issued device IP address during working hours.
- Reference keywords: Personal notes contain the phrase "next week announcement" matching the internal memo language.
