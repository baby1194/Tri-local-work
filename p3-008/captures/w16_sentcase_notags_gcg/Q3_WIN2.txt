Forensic findings
1) Domain context:
Equity markets operate on publicly disclosed information that is available to all participants simultaneously. Companies announce material corporate events, such as acquisitions, through official regulatory filings and press releases distributed via established channels like the SEC's EDGAR system and major newswires. Market participants access this information through public data feeds, brokerage platforms, and financial news services. Standard compliance controls include surveillance systems that monitor trading activity against public announcement timelines, insider trading restricted lists, and mandatory pre-clearance procedures for employees of financial institutions. These systems flag trades executed on material nonpublic information by comparing transaction timestamps against disclosure dates, analyzing unusual patterns in order routing, and cross-referencing trades with employee communications and data access logs to ensure all trading activity aligns with public knowledge availability.

2) What occurred:
The actor accessed a dated corporate communication within a business channel and extracted acquisition details from a named document. The actor recorded the announcement date and target company identifiers into a local text file. The actor identified a named brokerage account and reviewed the account's position holdings and order history. The actor calculated order sizes based on the account's historical trading patterns. The actor prepared multiple small orders across different security identifiers related to the acquisition target. The actor scheduled order submissions for staggered time intervals to avoid concentration within a single transaction window. The actor executed the orders through a third-party execution venue instead of the primary brokerage platform. The actor removed the local text file and cleared the channel's message history after order completion.

3) Materials/access observed:
A dated corporate communication within a business channel containing acquisition details. A named document with target company identifiers. A local text file created on the actor's workstation. A named brokerage account with position and order history access. A third-party execution venue account. The channel's message history log. System audit trails for file creation and deletion events.

4) Indicators & artifacts:
The dated corporate communication and its message ID within the business channel. The named document filename and its metadata. The local text file path and its creation/deletion timestamps. The named brokerage account identifier. Order records showing multiple small trades in acquisition-related securities executed in staggered intervals. The third-party execution venue account identifier and transaction logs. The channel's message deletion log entries. System audit records for file operations and account access.
