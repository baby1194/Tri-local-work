Forensic findings
1) Domain context: Jury selection (voir dire) is a standard civil and criminal procedure in which the court and counsel question prospective jurors to assess their impartiality. Ordinary practice permits questioning only through formal court mechanisms: submitted juror questionnaires, courtroom questioning, and limited peremptory challenges. Records are maintained by the court's clerk of court, and juror identities are protected by privacy rules and confidentiality seals. Communications with jurors outside court are subject to rules of judicial conduct and professional ethics. These controls exist to preserve the integrity of the adjudicative process.

2) What occurred: The actor reviewed publicly available court filings to identify a pending criminal case and its scheduled trial. Using a search engine, the actor located the prospective juror list and identified a specific individual. The actor then created a cover identity on a social media platform and initiated contact with the target juror, posing as a neighbor seeking information. The actor established a rapport and, after multiple exchanges, made an offer of financial compensation in exchange for influencing deliberations. Payment was to be made through a third-party intermediary, using a prepaid card purchased with cash. The actor then instructed the intermediary to dispose of the device and communication logs used to establish contact.

3) Materials/access observed: A court docket listing the prospective juror list, a social media account registered under a fictitious name, a prepaid payment card with no identifying metadata, and a set of messaging transcripts between the actor's intermediary and the target juror.

4) Indicators & artifacts: Creation of a new social media account with no prior history; sequential messaging pattern from the intermediary to the target juror; purchase of a prepaid card with cash; disposal of a communication device; and the absence of any legitimate court-authorized contact with the juror.
