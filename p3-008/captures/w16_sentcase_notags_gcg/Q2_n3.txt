Forensic findings
1) Domain context:

This matter concerns credentialing documents in higher education and their verification. A university transcript records courses, grades, credit hours, term dates, institutional identifiers, and registrar seals. A diploma states the degree conferred, date, and signatures of authorized officers. Legitimate verification relies on the registrar's office, sealed/letterhead documents, unique barcodes or QR codes, and cross-referencing with the institution's records. Background-verification channels include employer-initiated calls to the registrar, secure portals, and document-authentication services that check watermark paper, font metadata, and serial-number registration. Ordinary controls include institution-issued letterhead, non-reproducible seals, and digital registries linking a named account to a dated record.

2) What occurred:

The actor obtained a transcript and diploma template matching the target degree. They populated course titles, grades, credit hours, term dates, and a student identifier matching the named account. They added a registrar name, a seal impression, and a date field consistent with the claimed graduation year. The diploma page included a degree title, conferral date, two signature lines, and an institutional logo. To pass verification, the actor prepared responses to registrar calls, ensured the student identifier aligned with the background-verification record, and used a dated record whose metadata matched the claimed timeline.

3) Materials/access observed:

A named account and a dated record consistent with the claimed enrollment window; a transcript file containing course, grade, and credit-hour fields; a diploma page with degree title, date, and signature lines; an institutional seal impression; letterhead styling matching the target institution; and a background-verification file listing the claimed degree and date.

4) Indicators & artifacts:

Transcript and diploma files with matching student identifiers and dates; registrar-seal impressions on both documents; letterhead and font metadata consistent with a single preparation session; a background-verification record referencing a dated record; call-script notes prepared for registrar verification; and no digital registry entry linking the named account to a dated record at the issuing institution.
