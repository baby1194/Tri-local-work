Forensic findings
1) Domain context:
Kidney transplantation is governed by an internationally coordinated allocation system. Patient needs are assessed through standardized medical scoring (e.g., waitlist time, antibody mismatch, urgency) and registered with national transplant registries. Donor suitability requires medical and psychological evaluation, including proof of altruistic or deceased status. Living donation is documented through witnessed consent forms, financial-impact assessments, and anti-coercion interviews. Organs move through regulated courier chains with chain-of-custody logs, tissue-type matching, and reporting to monitoring bodies (e.g., UNOS/OPTN, NHSBT, or equivalent). Payment-for-organ schemes are prohibited; all transfers require audit trails from consent through implantation.

2) What occurred:
The actor collected a roster of individuals with documented high debt loads (e.g., medical arrears, consumer loans) and identified them as donor candidates. They drafted recruitment scripts and "donor package" brochures that framed transplantation as a debt-relief mechanism, listing specific compensation amounts and loan-forgiveness promises. Consent forms were templated with pre-filled donor names, dates, and signatures, then signed by donors under time pressure; several forms showed missing independent-witness lines and absent psychological-clearance stamps. Medical records were altered to downplay donor comorbidities (e.g., blood pressure, renal function) so candidates passed screening; the actor edited laboratory result fields in clinic systems and attached fabricated clearance letters on letterhead of a partner clinic. Recipient demand was routed through two overseas clinics; patient files were anonymized using codenames and transferred via encrypted email, while payment flows were described as "medical fee advances" in wire-transfer memos. Authority notifications were bypassed by filing incomplete registration packets with regional offices and using an expired broker license referenced on one submission.

3) Materials/access observed:
- A donor prospect spreadsheet (columns: name, debt amount, lender, contact, health flags).
- Consent form templates with pre-stamped witness fields and removable signature lines.
- Edited laboratory result documents (values overwritten; metadata inconsistent with upload timestamps).
- Two clinic letterheads used on clearance letters; one letterhead belonged to a clinic with no recorded partnership.
- Encrypted email threads codenaming recipients and clinics.
- Wire-transfer memos using "medical fee advance" descriptors; recipient accounts registered to third parties.
- An expired broker/agent license scanned and attached to a submission packet.
- Access credentials to a clinic appointment-scheduling interface and a laboratory results portal.
