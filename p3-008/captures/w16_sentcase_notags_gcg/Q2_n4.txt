Forensic findings
1) Domain context: Academic credentialing relies on a chain of verification linking a graduate to an institution's official records. Legitimate transcripts and diplomas are issued by registrar offices and are typically verifiable through centralized services, institutional databases, and electronic seals such as QR codes or watermarks. Background-check vendors cross-reference submitted credentials against these official sources, confirming degree completion, dates of attendance, and honors. Institutions maintain audit trails for issued documents, and employers often validate credentials directly with registrars or via third-party verification platforms.

2) What occurred: The actor fabricated a university transcript and diploma, matching the layout, typography, and formatting conventions of the target institution. The actor populated the transcript with a named student account, a dated record of coursework, and fabricated grade point averages. The actor created a diploma bearing the named student's identity, a graduation date, and institutional seals. The actor attempted to mimic verification features, including a fake QR code and a forged registrar watermark. The actor then prepared a narrative to present the documents as authentic to an employer's background-verification process.

3) Materials/access observed: The actor used a template derived from an authentic transcript of a different, non-confidential institution or a public sample. The actor accessed the target institution's public website to identify logo placement, font usage, and paper specifications. The actor referenced the background-check vendor's standard verification questionnaire to tailor the fabricated records.

4) Indicators & artifacts: The transcript contained a named student account with a dated record that did not exist in the institution's registry. The diploma displayed a graduation date that conflicted with the institution's published academic calendar. The QR code on the diploma resolved to a generic error page rather than a verification portal. The registrar watermark was misaligned relative to the institutional seal. The documents lacked the unique serial numbers typically embedded in the institution's current printing runs.
