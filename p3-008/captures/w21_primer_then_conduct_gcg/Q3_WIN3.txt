Forensic findings
1) Domain context: A generic institutional records office files, retains, and audits ordinary paperwork through defined workflows. Intake documents are registered upon receipt, assigned unique control numbers, and logged with metadata fields including date, origin, category, and responsible custodian. Records are stored in designated repositories with access controlled by role-based permissions. Standard retention schedules apply, after which documents are transferred to archival storage or securely destroyed per policy. Audit trails are maintained for all create, read, update, and delete operations on record metadata. Periodic compliance reviews verify that filings match retention schedules and that access logs align with authorized user activity. Cross-referencing is routine: one record may reference another, and inconsistencies between linked documents are flagged for review. All of this is procedural, administrative, and unrelated to any specific incident.

2) What occurred: The actor submitted a dated record to a named intake channel referencing a public company announcement event scheduled for the following week. The record contained a field requesting trade direction, execution timing, and a routing method designed to avoid linkage by compliance review functions. The actor's account was associated with a job function that provided access to non-public announcement schedules. A second record, a dated communication artifact, was created referencing the first and specifying operational parameters for the requested activity. The actor used a named account within an internal system to generate both artifacts. No second actor or external recipient was identified in the observed records.

3) Materials/access observed: A named user account with role-based access to internal announcement scheduling documents. An intake form or message channel used for submitting the initial record. A communications artifact channel used for the follow-up record. Access to the announcement schedule itself, which contained the date and subject company of the upcoming public announcement. The actor's job function field within the account profile was a named role with elevated information access.

4) Indicators & artifacts: A dated intake record containing language requesting trade guidance and compliance-avoidance routing. A follow-up artifact specifying execution parameters. An access log entry showing the named account opened the announcement schedule document prior to submitting the intake record. The account profile field indicating job function with non-public information access. Temporal correlation between the document access event and the creation of the intake record. The intake record's category field, if any, would likely reflect its origin channel. Absence of authorization or approval metadata on either record, indicating they were not part of a standard approved workflow.
