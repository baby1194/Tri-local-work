Forensic findings
1) Domain context:
Institutional records offices maintain transcripts and diplomas through controlled document management systems. Transcripts are issued on letterhead with a unique control number, date of issue, registrar signature, and a machine-readable barcode or QR code linking to the issuing system's audit trail. Diplomas carry sequential serial numbers, a registrar seal, and often a microperforation or embedded security thread. Records retention policies require original issue events to be logged in an immutable audit ledger. Auditing typically cross-references the control number against the enrollment database, validates the seal and serial range, and confirms the issuance timestamp falls within the student's attendance window. Verification channels include direct registrar hotlines, encrypted verification portals, and third-party verification services that query the original issue ledger rather than the document itself.

2) What occurred:
The actor produced a transcript document bearing an institutional letterhead, a fabricated course sequence, a grading scale, and a cumulative grade field. The actor set the issue date within a plausible attendance window and assigned a control number consistent with sequential patterns observed in legitimate records. The actor produced a diploma bearing a serial number, a registrar seal, and an engraved-style signature block. The actor selected a registrar name and title consistent with the institution's organizational structure. The actor formatted both documents to match the institution's standard layout, including font, margin, and seal placement. The actor prepared a response plan for registrar verification calls, scripting answers consistent with the fabricated control numbers and issue dates. The actor identified the employer's verification channel and prepared the documents for submission through that channel.

3) Materials/access observed:
The actor used a word processing application with custom fonts matching the institution's document templates. The actor used image manipulation software to replicate the registrar seal and produce an engraved-style signature. The actor had access to publicly available samples of the institution's transcripts and diplomas. The actor had access to the institution's registrar contact information and verification portal URL. The actor used a named account on a document sharing channel to transmit the fabricated documents to the employing entity. The actor used a dated record entry in a personal notes file tracking the sequence of fabricated documents produced and the verification events anticipated.

4) Indicators & artifacts:
The transcript's control number does not appear in the issuing system's audit ledger. The issue date falls outside the student's enrollment window. The grading pattern shows statistical anomalies inconsistent with the institution's published grade distributions. The diploma's serial number falls outside the allocated range for the institution's issuing period. The seal's edge definition and ink density differ from original seal impressions. The documents' metadata indicates recent creation with a general-purpose word processor rather than the institution's document management system. The actor's scripted verification responses contain statements that contradict the issuing ledger when cross-referenced by a diligent registrar.
