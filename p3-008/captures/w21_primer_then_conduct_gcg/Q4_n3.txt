Forensic findings
1) Domain context: A generic institutional records office receives documents through intake clerks, assigns each item a control number, dates the entry, and files it under a standard classification. Retention follows a fixed schedule: working files stay open for a defined period, then move to archival storage at regular intervals, with disposal only after an approved review. Audit trails record who created, read, modified, or deleted each record, and periodic spot checks verify that the chain of custody is intact. Files travel between desks by internal routing, and any external transmission is logged with sender, recipient, date, and reference number. The system is designed so that a third party can reconstruct, from the records alone, what paperwork existed, in what order, and which hands it passed through, without needing external testimony.

2) What occurred: The actor opened a set of correspondence items addressed to multiple separate property-management parties and filed them under a shared reference heading. Within these items, the actor completed fields identifying each recipient property, the corresponding neighborhood or parcel grouping, and a proposed adjustment to a monetary amount per unit. The actor entered a second set of items describing how the geographic parcel groupings were to be allocated, one per recipient, and marked each allocation as mutually exclusive. A third set of items defined a set of consequences to be applied to any party whose monetary figure fell below the common figure; these items cross-referenced the recipient accounts. The actor then recorded communication entries in a channel not routed through the standard logging system, with no assigned control number and no retained copy in the records office; the channel entries referenced the same monetary figures and parcel groupings but used abbreviated or substituted terms.

3) Materials/access observed: A named account, held by the actor, with access to the filing system and to the unlogged communication channel. Dated records for each correspondence item, each with a control number. A parcel-grouping table listing neighborhood identifiers. A set of cross-referenced items tying recipient accounts to the defined consequences. The unlogged channel's entry history, accessible to the actor but absent from the audit trail.

4) Indicators & artifacts: Absence of control numbers on a cluster of entries sharing identical monetary values across multiple recipient accounts. A parcel-grouping table in which neighborhood identifiers are partitioned one-to-one across named parties. Cross-referenced consequence items tied to a below-common-figure condition. Communication entries in an unlogged channel that mirror the values and groupings in the filed items while using abbreviated or substituted language. A dated-record gap in the standard audit trail coinciding with the period in which the unlogged entries were made.
