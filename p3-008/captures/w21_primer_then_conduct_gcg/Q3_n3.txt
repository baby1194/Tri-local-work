Forensic findings

1) Domain context:

An institutional records office organizes business paperwork by matter, date, and custodian. Incoming documents are intake-stamped, assigned a unique record identifier, and routed to the responsible unit. Files are retained in active, semi-active, and archive tiers per the retention schedule, with periodic audits verifying completeness, access logs, and disposition. Each record captures the document type, creation date, author, approver, distribution list, and version history. Access to the repository is role-based, logged, and reviewed quarterly. Documents marked confidential carry handling restrictions and are tracked separately from routine administrative files.

2) What occurred:

The actor created an intake note referencing a public company's planned acquisition and its scheduled announcement date. The actor then drafted a trading recommendation document, specifying the instrument to be purchased, the entry timing relative to the announcement, and a distribution routing path intended to obscure the actor's role. The document was versioned twice: an initial draft and a revised copy with altered routing fields. Both versions were saved under the actor's personal workspace directory rather than the standard matter folder. No approval workflow was initiated. The actor did not file the document through the intake stamp system, and no record identifier was assigned.

3) Materials/access observed:

- An intake note (undated, unassigned record ID) referencing a public company name and an announcement date field.
- A trading recommendation draft (version 1) containing an instrument identifier field, a timing field, and a routing field naming an unnamed account.
- A revised trading recommendation (version 2) with the routing field altered to an additional unnamed account; the version history showed the actor's credentials as the sole author.
- File metadata placed both documents in the actor's personal workspace directory, outside the standard matter folder structure.
- No approval record, no distribution log entry, and no compliance routing stamp were observed on either version.

4) Indicators & artifacts:

- Absence of a record identifier on documents referencing material non-public information.
- Version history showing iterative edits to routing and distribution fields by a single actor.
- File placement in a personal directory rather than a controlled matter folder.
- Timing field referencing a future corporate announcement date prior to public disclosure.
- No intake stamp, no approval workflow artifact, and no compliance log entry associated with the document set.
