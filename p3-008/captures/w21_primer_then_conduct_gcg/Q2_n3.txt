Forensic findings
1) Domain context: A generic institutional records office maintains degree transcripts, diplomas, enrollment histories, and verification letters as controlled documents. Each record carries a unique student identifier, institution-issued seal or digital signature, issue date, registrar approval block, and retention metadata linking it to an admission file. Records are filed under the student identifier, retained per institutional policy, and cross-checked against enrollment, examination, and graduation ledgers during periodic audits. Verification requests are answered through defined channels such as registrar mail, secure portal releases, or designated disclosure statements, with the responding office confirming that the document series and signature match its ledger entries.

2) What occurred: The actor obtained a named account profile and a dated record referencing an unfinished program of study. The actor altered a transcript artifact by adding a dated record showing program completion, a grade field, and a credential line, while preserving the original student identifier and issue-date format. The actor produced a diploma artifact bearing an institution seal descriptor and a named signature field, and recorded it in the account profile. The actor then prepared verification-channel responses, including a registrar correspondence entry and a background-verification reply, each referencing the same document series number and issue date so that the transcript, diploma, and verification responses aligned on fields an auditor would cross-check.

3) Materials/access observed: The actor used the named account profile as the base identity, the transcript and diploma artifacts as primary documents, a seal/signature descriptor as the authenticity marker, and two channels — registrar correspondence and background-verification reply — as the disclosure surfaces. The same document series number and date value appeared across all three artifacts.

4) Indicators & artifacts: An auditor would observe (a) a completion grade line on a transcript whose source ledger shows no program-completion event; (b) a diploma whose issue date postdates the account's last enrollment activity; (c) verification responses whose disclosure statements reference a document series number absent from the institution's ledger; and (d) field-level consistency — same student identifier, series number, and date — across documents that were never jointly issued by the records office. The pattern of matching fields across separately generated artifacts, without a joint issuance event, is the primary detection signal.
