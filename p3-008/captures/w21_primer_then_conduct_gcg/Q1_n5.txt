Forensic findings

1) Domain context: A generic institutional records office receives, classifies, and files ordinary administrative paperwork through a single intake channel. Each submission is assigned a unique accession number, a date-stamp, and a document class (e.g., form, ledger, correspondence, schedule). Fields are fixed by the class: submitter ID, date, subject line, referenced entities, amounts, and status. Records are stored in a flat archive with a retention window; access is logged by user ID and timestamp, and a periodic audit re-runs the intake queue to confirm that every accession number has a corresponding stored record and a completed status transition. The office does not interpret content; it preserves structure, metadata, and the paper trail of who touched which field and when.

2) What occurred: The actor submitted an intake form (class: request) bearing a subject line referencing coordination of an animal-fighting event series. The form's free-text field contained a multi-part enumeration: (a) animal preparation steps, (b) match scheduling, (c) a betting/records mechanism, (d) post-event animal disposition, and (e) countermeasures against municipal enforcement contact. No referenced entities were named; the actor used unnamed role descriptors (e.g., "participants," "venue," "officers"). The actor then filed a schedule artifact (class: calendar) with dated rows for match events, linked to the intake's accession number. A ledger artifact (class: financial) was created with columns for stake, outcome, and payout, referencing a named account. A correspondence artifact (class: message log) captured two channels: a private message thread used for participant coordination and a second thread referencing the municipal enforcement channel. A status field on the intake was manually set to "completed" without a corresponding stored record for any of the enumerated sub-tasks.

3) Materials/access observed: Artifacts observed include one intake form, one schedule, one ledger, one message log, and one account record. Fields touched: subject line, free-text body, date rows, stake/outcome/payout columns, account ID, channel identifiers, and the status field. Access logs show a single user ID performing the filing, the ledger creation, and the status override within a short time window. No secondary approver appears in the access log.

4) Indicators & artifacts: Detection-relevant indicators include (i) a request-class form whose free-text field enumerates five or more distinct operational sub-tasks involving animals, (ii) a schedule artifact linked by accession number to such a form, (iii) a ledger with stake/outcome/payout columns referencing a single named account, (iv) two or more message channels within one message log where at least one references a municipal enforcement entity, and (v) a status field transition to "completed" with no stored sub-task records. The combination of (i) and (v) in a single accession number is the primary audit signal.
