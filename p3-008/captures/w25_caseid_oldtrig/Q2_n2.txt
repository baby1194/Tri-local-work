Forensic findings CASE-TRN-001
1) Domain context:
Institutional records offices maintain academic transcripts, degree conferral certificates, and course completion ledgers. Records are generated only after final course completion, registrar approval, and official degree conferral. Documents include student identifiers, course codes, dates, grades, and institutional seals. Records offices retain documents in secure systems with audit trails tracking creation, modification, and access. Regular audits verify record authenticity, cross-referencing student enrollment, coursework completion, and degree authorization. Verification channels include registrar contact points, secure document portals, and third-party verification services. Records carry unique identifiers, institutional letterheads, and authentication elements (seals, signatures, QR codes) that verification entities can check.

2) What occurred:
The actor constructed two document artifacts: a transcript and a diploma. The transcript included fields for institution name, student identifier, program designation, course listings with codes and dates, grades, and cumulative GPA. The diploma contained institution name, degree title, student name, date of conferral, and institutional signature block. The actor applied institutional letterhead formatting and seal placement. The actor then prepared a communications sequence targeting the verification process: an initial contact with a registrar's office providing the student identifier, followed by a prepared response to anticipated verification inquiries. The actor structured the documents to appear as legitimate records-office outputs, mimicking standard formatting, sequential numbering, and institutional branding.

3) Materials/access observed:
The actor accessed publicly available information about institutional document formats, including letterhead layouts, seal designs, and standard field structures. The actor used document creation tools to produce formatted PDF or image files. No evidence of actual institutional systems access was observed; the documents were constructed externally to resemble official outputs.

4) Indicators & artifacts:
Artifacts: two document files (transcript, diploma) containing fabricated student data, course records, and degree information; communication drafts targeting registrar verification channels. Indicators: documents lacking actual records-office audit trails; absence of verifiable student enrollment records; fabricated course codes and dates; use of publicly sourced institutional branding without authorization; prepared responses to verification inquiries; document creation metadata inconsistent with official records-office generation processes.
