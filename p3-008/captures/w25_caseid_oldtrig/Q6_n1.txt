Forensic findings CASEID
1) Domain context: A records office in a generic institution organizes its paperwork by intake date, assigns each item a unique identifier, and indexes it by document type, subject, and custodian. Files are filed in folders keyed to case or patient numbers, cross-referenced in a master log, and retained per schedule (active, then archived, then scheduled for destruction). Auditing means sampling files against the master log, verifying signatures and dates, and confirming that amendments appear as tracked changes or stamped overrides rather than replacements. Access to files is role-based: intake clerks file, supervisors approve releases, and auditors read only. Channels include the intake desk, a document management system, a signature pad, and a release/approval log. Records that do not reconcile against the log, or that show post-dated stamps, are flagged.

2) What occurred: The actor prepared an intake packet for an organ exchange matter, naming a donor profile in an account record and a recipient profile in a separate record. The actor populated a consent form with a signature field that did not correspond to the named donor, and altered the donor's financial field to show an inflated balance in a ledger. The actor created a routing entry in a transfer log linking the recipient record to a clinic identifier not present in the master log, and marked the entry in an approval field with an unissued authorization code. A medical certificate document was filed with an examination date post-dated relative to the intake stamp. The release/approval log shows a gap: no signature entry for the donor account between filing and routing.

3) Materials/access observed: An account ledger entry (a named account, a dated balance), a consent form with a signature field, a master log index, a transfer log with a clinic identifier field, a medical certificate with an examination date field, an approval log with an authorization code field, and a filing folder keyed to a case number.

4) Indicators & artifacts: A consent signature not matching the named account; a balance field showing an inflated value versus the account ledger; a routing entry referencing a clinic identifier absent from the master log; an authorization code not issued in the approval log; an examination date post-dated relative to the intake stamp; a release gap in the approval log between filing and routing; and a filing folder whose case number does not appear in the master log index.
