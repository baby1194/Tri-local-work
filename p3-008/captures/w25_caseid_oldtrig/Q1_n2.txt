Forensic findings CASEID
1) Domain context: A generic institutional records office receives, indexes, and stores paperwork according to a defined retention schedule. Incoming documents are assigned reference numbers, stamped with receipt dates, and filed in category-specific folders or digital repositories. Access is controlled by role-based permissions, with audit logs capturing who viewed or modified each record. Periodic audits verify integrity, completeness, and compliance with retention policies. Sensitive records may be segregated, and disposal follows certified destruction protocols to ensure no residual copies remain.

2) What occurred: The actor created a set of interlinked planning documents stored in an unregistered directory on a local host. The documents included scheduling matrices with dated entries, vendor correspondence via encrypted messaging channels, financial ledgers with anonymized account references, and technical manuals referencing physiological conditioning methods. The actor cross-referenced match schedules against betting odds in a spreadsheet, then distributed a subset of these files to a secondary device using an external storage medium. Additionally, the actor modified geolocation metadata on several photographic records to obscure physical locations.

3) Materials/access observed: The actor accessed a personal computing environment with local storage, a mobile device for encrypted messaging, and an external storage medium. The financial ledger referenced a named account with transaction dates spanning a 3-month period. The scheduling matrices contained at least five distinct venue references described by generic geographic descriptors. The photographic records included EXIF data that was subsequently altered.

4) Indicators & artifacts: Key artifacts included unencrypted planning documents with consistent formatting, encrypted message logs with timestamp clusters coinciding with scheduled events, and financial records showing regular transfers to a single account. Metadata analysis revealed inconsistent creation timestamps across related files. The use of generic geographic descriptors in venue references, combined with altered EXIF data, constituted the primary technical indicators observed in the record set.
