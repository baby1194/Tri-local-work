Forensic findings CASEID
1) Domain context: Institutional records offices maintain credential verification through centralized databases that link student identifiers to academic histories. Retention policies typically preserve transcripts, diplomas, and enrollment records for seven to ten years post-graduation, with digital copies stored in encrypted repositories and physical documents in climate-controlled archives. Regular audits cross-reference internal student information systems against registrar outputs to detect inconsistencies in dates, credit hours, or grading patterns. Verification protocols involve authenticated API endpoints that respond to employer queries with cryptographically signed confirmations, ensuring that any requested validation can be traced back to authorized database entries. Standard procedures include quarterly reconciliation between tuition payments, course registrations, and degree conferral records to maintain institutional integrity.

2) What occurred: The actor constructed a complete academic record package containing a transcript listing twelve courses across three semesters with a 3.8 GPA, a diploma bearing the institution's letterhead and seal with a named account holder's details and a dated record of conferral, and supporting documentation including an enrollment verification letter. The actor replicated specific fields including course titles, credit hours, grade points, and cumulative GPA calculations that aligned with the institution's standard grading scale. Channels observed included a document preparation service, email exchanges with a named individual requesting specific formatting requirements, and preparation of verification responses that matched the institution's standard confirmation letter template.

3) Materials/access observed: The actor utilized institutional branding materials including logo files, seal images, and letterhead templates. Access to the institution's standard verification response format was evidenced by the precise matching of field labels and response structure. No authenticated access to the actual student information system was demonstrated; instead, the materials reflected knowledge of standard institutional formats rather than direct database access.

4) Indicators & artifacts: Key indicators included verification responses that matched the institution's template structure but could not be traced to any actual student record in the information system. The transcript showed course registrations without corresponding tuition payment records. The diploma serial number fell outside the institution's active numbering sequence. Email metadata revealed the documents originated from an external email address rather than the institution's official domain. The background verification response lacked the cryptographic signature typically present in legitimate institutional confirmations.
