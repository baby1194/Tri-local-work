1) Domain context: A generic institutional records office maintains a centralized archive of correspondence, case files, and procedural paperwork. Incoming materials are registered upon receipt, assigned unique accession numbers, and routed to the relevant department. Records are retained for defined periods according to a retention schedule, and access is logged through access control lists tied to authorized roles. The archive office supports audit functions by preserving chain-of-custody metadata: timestamps, handler identifiers, and transfer logs. Regular audits compare physical holdings against the electronic index, flag discrepancies, and require documented justification for any record alteration, destruction, or transfer. The intake, processing, and archival workflow is designed so that every action leaves a traceable audit entry, and deviations from standard procedure are reported to an oversight body. This context describes ordinary archival practice; it contains no facts from the intake.
