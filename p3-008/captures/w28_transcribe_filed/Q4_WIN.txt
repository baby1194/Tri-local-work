1) Domain context: An institutional records office receives, indexes, and retains documents in sealed series assigned accession numbers. Each series contains dated records with standardized fields for origin, recipient, date, and summary, filed in chronological order within a master index. Access is logged per custodian, with read and write events time-stamped. Retention schedules govern how long each series is kept before archival transfer or destruction under an approved disposition code. Auditors reconstruct conduct by tracing the chain of custody across index entries, access logs, and correspondence folders. Communications are cataloged by channel (internal mail, phone, meeting) and cross-referenced against the master index to identify patterns of interaction. Findings are expressed solely in terms of filed documents, fields, and channels, using neutral descriptors for all parties and values, so that a reviewer can verify each claim against the physical or electronic record without reliance on narrative interpretation.

2) What occurred: A set of dated records was filed in a correspondence series, each bearing a sender field (a named account) and a recipient field (multiple distinct named accounts). The records' summary field described coordination among the recipient accounts concerning pricing parameters, territorial allocation, and response to deviation from the agreed parameters. A subsequent record's summary field described a communication channel designated as low-trace, with a field indicating the channel was selected to minimize discoverability of the agreement. A later dated record in the same series documented enforcement: a field identified a deviation by one named account, and a summary field described collective response measures directed at that account. All records shared a common origin field (a named account acting as coordinator) and were filed in sequence without gaps in the master index.

3) Materials/access observed: The records resided in a sealed correspondence series, with each document assigned an accession number and a filing date. Fields included: sender account identifier, recipient account identifiers (multiple), date, summary text, channel designator, and a boolean field indicating trace-minimization intent. Access logs showed the coordinator account performing read and write operations on all records in the series. A separate access log entry showed the coordinator account querying the channel designator field across multiple records on a single date, consistent with reviewing which low-trace channels had been used. No custodian outside the coordinator account had write access to the series during the retention period.

4) Indicators & artifacts: (a) Multiple dated records in one series with a common sender field and multiple distinct recipient fields, where summary text references collective pricing parameters. (b) A field indicating channel selection for trace-minimization, present on records whose summary references the pricing parameters. (c) A record whose summary field documents a response to a deviation by one recipient account, filed within a short interval after a record identifying the deviation. (d) An access-log pattern in which the coordinator account performs bulk reads of the channel designator field, concentrated on a single date, across the series. (e) Absence of any third-party custodian with write access to the series, combined with a single-origin pattern across all accession numbers.
