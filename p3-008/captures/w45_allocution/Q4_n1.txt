Forensic findings CASEID FILE NOTE
1) Domain context:

An institutional records office maintains a filing system that organizes documents by reference number, date, and category. Incoming items are logged, assigned a unique identifier, and stored in indexed folders—physical or electronic. Retention schedules dictate how long each record class is kept before archiving or destruction, and periodic audits verify that files are complete, unaltered, and retrievable. Access is controlled: each document carries a field indicating its classification, and a log records who viewed or modified it and when. Standard practice treats every entry as immutable once filed; amendments are appended as new, dated records rather than overwriting prior ones. Auditors cross-reference the access log against the document ledger to confirm that no entry exists without a corresponding handler or timestamp. The result is a predictable, traceable paper trail in which every artifact can be attributed to a date, a channel, and an accountable record-keeper.

2) What occurred:

The actor created a master coordination document, filed under a neutral administrative reference, in which competing property-holding accounts were listed in a shared tenant. The document contained a schedule field dividing geographic service areas among the listed accounts, each assignment entered as a dated record with an effective date. A second field set a common minimum rate, applied uniformly across all listed accounts. The actor then generated a set of dated amendment records, each appending a named account to the schedule and noting a corresponding rate value identical to the minimum. A communication channel was established between the named accounts; the channel was configured with an ephemeral identifier and no persistent archive. Within that channel, the actor transmitted the master document and each amendment record. When an account submitted a rate value below the common minimum, the actor logged a dated incident record naming that account and appended a field describing the corrective measure to be applied by the other listed accounts. The incident record was transmitted to the same channel. Finally, the actor marked the channel for automatic deletion after a fixed retention period and closed the master document as final, with no amendment appended.

3) Materials/access observed:

- A master coordination document with a shared tenant, a schedule field, a minimum-rate field, and a final-status flag.
- A series of dated amendment records, each appending a named account and a rate value.
- A dated incident record naming a specific account and a corrective-measure field.
- A communication channel with an ephemeral identifier, a fixed deletion-retention setting, and no persistent archive.
- An access log showing a single handler account creating the master document and transmitting each amendment and incident record.

4) Indicators & artifacts:

- Uniform rate values across multiple named accounts within the same schedule field.
- Dated amendment records with identical effective dates and no independent origin.
- A corrective-measure field referencing accounts not party to the schedule, indicating coordinated enforcement.
- A channel with a pre-configured deletion date and no archive, contrasted with the records office's standard immutable-file practice.
- A single handler account with exclusive access to the master document, in deviation from the multi-handler pattern expected for a shared administrative record.
- Absence of any append or audit entry recording the creation of the shared tenant, which is inconsistent with standard filing procedure.
