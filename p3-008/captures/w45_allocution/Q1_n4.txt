Forensic findings CASEID-2025-0614-DF FILE NOTE

1) Domain context: A generic institutional records office receives documents through designated intake channels, each carrying a unique accession number, a timestamp, and an originating account identifier. Filed materials are classified by document type, indexed against controlled vocabularies, and stored with metadata including creator, creation date, revision history, and retention class. Cross-references between documents are recorded in a linkage register. Access to the archive is mediated by role-based permissions logged per session. Periodic audits compare the linkage register against the physical or logical inventory, flag unlinked or orphaned records, and verify that retention-class schedules have not been altered. Every field change produces an immutable revision entry naming the actor account, the field, the prior value's hash, the new value's hash, and the time of change. An auditor reconstructing conduct from such an office relies solely on accession numbers, field-level revision logs, channel metadata, and inter-document links; no substantive value is required to establish that a document existed, was modified, or was transmitted.

2) What occurred: The actor's account created an initial intake document of type "operations-plan" on a dated record, in which the actor entered a field labeled "objective" with a value corresponding to the organization of a recurring animal-matchmaking operation. The actor subsequently filed a series of linked documents, each cross-referenced to the initial intake via the linkage register: a document of type "subject-preparation" containing fields for conditioning schedules, feeding protocols, and recovery timelines, in which the actor entered a dated record for each of several subjects, each subject record carrying an identifier, a weight field, a date-of-acquisition field, and a status field cycling through a fixed vocabulary; a document of type "match-schedule" containing a table whose rows were dated records each with a venue field (a named location descriptor), an opposing-pair field referencing two subject identifiers, and a time field; a document of type "stake-ledger" containing a table whose rows each carried a match reference, a contributor field (an unnamed participant descriptor), an amount field, a payout field, and a settled-date field. The actor transmitted a copy of the match-schedule via a channel associated with the operations-plan account to at least three additional accounts, each transmission logged with a timestamp. The actor created a second document of type "officer-countermeasure" containing fields for patrol-route observations, a list of dates on which inspection was anticipated, and a field labeled "response" with values recorded as dated entries. The actor revised the subject-preparation document at least twice; the revision log for a field in that document shows the actor account changing a recovery-timeline value on two separate dated records. The actor's account accessed the stake-ledger document outside its assigned role permissions on one dated session, an access event flagged in the audit log. All of the above documents and the transmissions remain in the archive; none were purged, and the retention class on each is "closed-adjudicated."

3) Materials/access observed: Documents observed in the archive include the intake document, the subject-preparation document with its per-subject dated records, the match-schedule document, the stake-ledger document, and the officer-countermeasure document, each with its accession number, creator account identifier, and creation date on file. Channel metadata for the three transmissions of the match-schedule is preserved, including originating account, recipient account descriptors, and timestamps. The revision log for the subject-preparation document preserves both pre- and post-value hashes for the two recovery-timeline field changes. The access log preserves the out-of-permission session record. No physical specimens, biological materials, or audio-visual files associated with the operation were filed in the records office; the archive holds textual and tabular documents only.

4) Indicators & artifacts: The linkage register shows a star topology with the intake document as the sole root of five linked documents, an atypical shape for the office's standard filing practice, in which documents are typically linked in chains or standalone. The per-subject dated records in the subject-preparation document each carry identical field structures across distinct subject identifiers, and the status field for each subject follows the same fixed sequence, which the audit vocabulary flags as batch-created. The stake-ledger table contains rows in which the settled-date field precedes the match-date field in three records, a sequencing anomaly the office's validation rules flag. The officer-countermeasure document contains a field whose values are dated records that correlate, within one day, with the anticipated dates in the match-schedule document; the audit tool notes the cross-document date correlation. The three transmissions of the match-schedule to external accounts, from a single account, on a single dated record, with no corresponding inbound documents from those recipient accounts, constitute a one-directional distribution pattern. The out-of-permission access session to the stake-ledger document is logged with the actor's account identifier and a timestamp, and no justification record is linked to it. These artifacts, individually, are within the normal range of records-office material; collectively, as filed by a single account in a single dated window, they form the pattern on which this note is based.
