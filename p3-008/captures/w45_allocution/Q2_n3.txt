Forensic findings CASEID FILE NOTE
1) Domain context: Institutional records offices manage official documents through controlled access, version tracking, and audit trails. Filing procedures require authentication of document provenance, verification of issuing authority signatures, and cross-referencing with central registries. Retention policies mandate storage of originals or certified copies in sealed archives, with access logs recording every retrieval or modification. Audit processes involve periodic reconciliation of student records against payment, enrollment, and graduation data, using cryptographic hashes and sequential numbering to detect tampering. Ordinary paperwork flows through intake, verification, cataloging, and final archiving, with each step timestamped and signed by authorized personnel.

2) What occurred: The actor created a document resembling an official academic transcript, populating fields for course titles, grades, dates of attendance, and cumulative grade point average. A second document mimicked a diploma, with fields for degree type, conferral date, and registrar's signature line. Both documents incorporated institutional letterhead, seal embossing, and registration number formats consistent with the target institution's templates. The actor then prepared a third document structured as a registrar correspondence, with fields for verification confirmation, contact details, and official endorsement stamp. These documents were transmitted via a channel posing as institutional email, using an account registered to appear as a staff member of the records office.

3) Materials/access observed: The actor used design software to replicate typographic elements and watermark patterns from publicly available versions of the institution's documents. A domain was registered to host verification pages, with DNS records configured to resolve to a server under the actor's control. The email account employed SPF and DKIM records mimicking the institution's mail server configuration. The registrar correspondence included a phone number associated with a VoIP service, and a physical address matching the institution's campus. The documents contained a registration number format matching the institution's sequential numbering scheme but with digits inconsistent with the year of attendance.

4) Indicators & artifacts: The transcript's cumulative grade point average field contained a value mathematically inconsistent with the individual course grades listed. The diploma's conferral date preceded the earliest course completion date on the transcript by two months. The registrar correspondence's email address used a subdomain structure not present in the institution's verified domain records. The VoIP phone number's carrier registration showed a creation date six weeks before the verification request. The domain's WHOIS record displayed a privacy service with a registration date matching the document creation timeline. The documents' PDF metadata included software identifiers inconsistent with the institution's known document generation systems.
