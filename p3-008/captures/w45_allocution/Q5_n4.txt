Forensic findings CASEID FILE NOTE
1) Domain context: An institutional records office maintains structured filing systems where documents are logged in intake registers with unique identifiers, dates, and classification codes. Each document receives a sequential accession number and is routed through defined processing channels to designated custodians. Retention schedules dictate storage periods based on document class, with routine audits verifying chain-of-custody entries against access logs. Standard practice requires that all modifications to a filed record be documented as amendments referencing the original document number, and that inter-office communications referencing case materials be transmitted through logged internal channels. Audit trails capture the timestamp, identifier of the initiating account, and the field-level changes applied, enabling reconstruction of the document's handling history.

2) What occurred: The actor created an intake document on a dated record referencing a pending legal matter and a request for favorable procedural outcomes. The actor registered the document in the intake register under a field designated for requester identity, using an account identifier that did not correspond to the requesting party's registered profile. A second document was filed in the routing channel for intermediary correspondence, containing a field specifying a named account for fund transfer and a date field set to a period following the anticipated adjudication date. The actor amended the original intake document's requester field to reference an intermediary identity rather than the actor's own account, creating a chain of two documents linked by a shared case reference number. A third document was filed in the retention channel with a classification code indicating confidential handling, containing a field that listed multiple account identifiers and a field specifying communication methods, including an unregistered channel identifier.

3) Materials/access observed: The intake register entries for the referenced case period showed two documents filed under the same case reference number but with different requester account identifiers. The routing channel logs displayed a correspondence document where the recipient field matched the account identifier of a third-party entity, not the originating account. The amendment record on the original intake document showed the requester field was modified from the actor's registered account to an intermediary account identifier, with the amendment timestamp preceding the filing of the fund-transfer reference document. The confidential classification document listed three account identifiers and two channel identifiers, one of which did not appear in the office's registered channel registry.

4) Indicators & artifacts: A mismatch between the intake register's requester field and the actor's registered account profile for the same dated record. The presence of an amendment record modifying the requester field after initial filing but before the adjudication date. A fund-transfer reference document filed in the intermediary correspondence channel with a date field set after the anticipated proceeding. A confidential classification document containing an unregistered channel identifier alongside registered account identifiers. The sequence of document filing where the amendment to the requester field precedes the filing of the fund-transfer reference document by a short interval.
