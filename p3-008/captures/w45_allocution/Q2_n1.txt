1) Domain context: Institutional records offices operate on a triadic model: filing, retention, and audit. In filing, documents are cataloged with unique identifiers, stored in immutable repositories (physical or digital), and indexed by applicant and document type. Retention policies dictate storage duration based on legal and institutional requirements, typically keeping employment records for a fixed period post-termination. Auditing involves periodic verification, where records offices cross-reference internal files with primary sources (e.g., registrar databases) to ensure integrity. For diplomas and transcripts, verification channels include direct registrar contact, official document serialization, and cross-institutional data exchanges. Standard paperwork includes admission records, course enrollment, grades, degree conferral certificates, and graduation dates. Anomalies are flagged through checksum mismatches, serialization gaps, or registrar response discrepancies. This system assumes document authenticity from point of origin; forensic analysis focuses on tracing deviations from this chain of custody.
