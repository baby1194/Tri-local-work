Forensic findings CASEID-FAKE-TRANSCRIPT-001 FILE NOTE
1) Domain context: Institutional records offices maintain centralized repositories of academic credentials, typically filed as digitized scans and metadata records in student information systems. Ordinary transcripts are issued on letterhead with standardized field layouts: institution name, student identifier, course titles, credit hours, grades, date of conferral, and registrar signature or seal. Diplomas bear the institution's seal, an issuance date, a unique certificate number, and authorized signatory blocks. Retention policies govern how long records are stored and in what form; audit trails capture who accessed or modified entries, with hash-verified copies for verification purposes. Accreditation bodies maintain public directories, and verification channels include direct registrar contacts, official portals, and third-party credential checkers, each leaving query logs that tie a verification request to a specific individual and date.

2) What occurred: The actor prepared two documents modeled on the layout of a named university. The first, a transcript, contained a student identifier field, an enrollment date field, a date-of-graduation field, a list of course fields with credit-hour and grade values, and a registrar attestation field. The second, a diploma, contained an institution name field, a degree field, a date-of-conferral field, a certificate-number field, and a signatory field. The actor transmitted both documents through a named communication channel to a named recipient account. The actor then composed a series of messages in the same channel describing how to obtain or replicate the institution's letterhead, seal, and verification portal behavior, and how to respond to registrar queries using a pre-prepared date-and-name field set matching the fabricated record.

3) Materials/access observed: The actor produced a transcript document and a diploma document, each bearing a named institution's identifier fields. A letterhead template file with the institution name and contact fields was observed. A seal image file matching the institution's known mark was observed. A set of name-and-date fields consistent with a conferral event was observed. The documents were transmitted through a named message channel to a named account. No authenticated registrar access was observed; instead, the actor's message fields referenced manual replication of verification responses.

4) Indicators & artifacts: A transcript document with a student identifier, enrollment date, graduation date, course-credit-grade field set, and registrar attestation field, bearing a named institution's letterhead. A diploma document with a certificate number, date of conferral, degree title, institution seal field, and signatory block, bearing the same letterhead. A letterhead template file containing the institution name and contact fields. A seal image file. A set of name-and-date field values consistent across both documents. Transmission records in a named channel showing the documents sent to a named recipient account. Message-field text in the same channel describing letterhead and seal replication and manual verification-response handling. No corresponding entry in the institution's student records system or verification portal query logs tied to the actor's identifier.
