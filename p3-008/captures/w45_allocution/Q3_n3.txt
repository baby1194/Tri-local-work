Forensic findings CASEID FILE NOTE
1) Domain context: In a generic institutional records office, documents are created at defined intake points (e.g., a signed form, an approved requisition, or a system-generated log), each carrying standard fields such as record ID, creator, timestamp, classification, and retention period. Documents are filed into controlled repositories where read, write, and export permissions are role-scoped and every access is journaled. Retention rules hold records for a defined period, after which disposition is logged; superseded versions are archived rather than deleted. Auditing operates through periodic reconciliation of the access journal against the permission registry and the document index, with sampled records re-verified for field integrity (hash or checksum) and lifecycle events (create, modify, export, archive). Discrepancies — orphaned versions, out-of-window edits, exports without corresponding approval fields — are flagged as record exceptions for follow-up.

2) What occurred: The actor prepared a dated record containing the terms of the requested matter (object, timing, and routing instructions) and filed it under a personal identifier within a shared repository. The actor then created a second dated record, an export of the first, addressed to a named account, transmitting the terms to that account via a non-journaled channel. A third dated record, an access journal entry, reflects a read event against the first document by the actor outside the normal retention-window workflow. A fourth dated record, a modification, altered the classification field of the first document from "restricted" to "unclassified" prior to the export. A fifth dated record, an approval field left blank on a routing form, shows no supervisor sign-off was attached to the export.

3) Materials/access observed: A dated record (the intake document) in a shared repository; a named account with export permissions; a non-journaled communication channel; a personal device used to compose the export; and read access to the classification field of the intake document. The actor's role was not listed in the permission registry for that repository at the time of the access event.

4) Indicators & artifacts: (a) A modification to a classification field on a document that had no pending workflow action. (b) An export event with no corresponding approval field populated on the routing form. (c) A read event outside the standard access window for the document's retention class. (d) A dated record in a non-journaled channel matching the terms of the intake document within a short time gap. (e) An account in the access journal not present in the permission registry for that repository at the time of the event. (f) The absence of an archived superseded version that would normally accompany a classification change.
