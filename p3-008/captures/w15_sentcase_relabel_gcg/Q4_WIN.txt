Forensic findings
1) Domain context: Residential rental markets are typically competitive, with landlords setting rents independently based on local demand, operating costs, and comparable properties. Standard industry practices include published pricing through listing services, tenant screening via background checks, and communication through documented channels such as email and property management platforms. Regulatory oversight generally involves tenant protection agencies, fair housing boards, and antitrust authorities that monitor market conduct. Property owners normally document agreements in leases, maintain financial records of income and expenses, and comply with local ordinances regarding rent stabilization, eviction procedures, and price increases. Market intelligence is shared through industry associations, real estate boards, and public data sources like assessor records and MLS listings.

2) What occurred: The actor initiated contact with multiple property owners in the same geographic region, proposing coordinated rent increases at specified intervals. The actor created a shared document titled "Market Adjustment Schedule" containing columns for property address, current rent, proposed rent, effective date, and owner contact information. The actor established a communication protocol using ephemeral messaging apps with auto-delete enabled, instructing participants to reference properties by address number only rather than owner names. The actor drafted a "Neighborhood Allocation" spreadsheet dividing properties into zones A through F, assigning each zone to a designated owner with corresponding rent floors. The actor recorded meeting minutes in a private folder labeled "General Updates" that documented verbal agreements on pricing targets and enforcement mechanisms for non-compliant participants.

3) Materials/access observed: The actor maintained access to a shared cloud storage repository containing the allocation spreadsheet, the market adjustment schedule, and meeting minutes. The actor used three separate device accounts: a primary business email, a secondary personal email, and a messaging platform account. The actor accessed property listing data from a commercial real estate database and cross-referenced it with county assessor records. Financial documents from participating properties, including lease files and payment records, were stored in a shared drive organized by property address.

4) Indicators & artifacts: Artifacts included the "Market Adjustment Schedule" document with columns for current and proposed rents; the "Neighborhood Allocation" spreadsheet with zone assignments and rent floors; meeting minutes documenting pricing discussions; ephemeral message logs showing auto-deletion settings; and a shared cloud storage folder with access permissions granted to multiple property owners. Communication patterns included coded references to properties, time-stamped messages coinciding with rent increase implementations across multiple addresses, and consistent messaging from the actor's accounts in the hours following property price changes.

5) Notes: The actor's communications used specific terminology including "market adjustment," "allocation," and "zone" to describe coordinated pricing. The actor maintained a log of properties that deviated from agreed pricing, with notes on follow-up actions. The actor's messaging account exhibited activity patterns consistent with the documented communication protocol, including regular check-ins with participants and reminders about upcoming pricing dates.
