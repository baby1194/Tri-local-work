Forensic findings
1) Domain context:
The commercial real estate industry operates through landlords, property managers, and leasing brokers who compete for tenants in distinct geographic markets. Ordinary market conduct involves each operator independently setting rents, negotiating lease terms, and marketing available units based on property condition, location, and prevailing market conditions. Standard compliance controls include antitrust compliance policies, independent decision-making protocols, and communication guidelines that discourage direct coordination on pricing with competitors. Routine industry interactions occur through neutral channels such as trade association events, listing services, and market data reports. Lease agreements, rent rolls, marketing materials, and pricing worksheets are maintained as standard business records, typically stored in property management systems with role-based access controls and audit logging.

2) What occurred:
The actor initiated coordination by contacting multiple competing landlords through private messaging channels to propose a mutual agreement on rent increases. The actor created a shared spreadsheet document named <RENT_COORDINATION_FILE> containing columns for property identifier, current rent, proposed rent increase percentage, and agreed effective date. The actor populated the proposed rent fields with values calculated to eliminate price competition. The actor then drafted a neighborhood division matrix in <MARKET_DIVISION_DOC> assigning specific properties to each participant with exclusivity notes. The actor established a penalty mechanism in <ENFORCEMENT_TERMS> specifying consequences for any participant who undercut the agreed pricing. Communication was routed through encrypted messaging platform <ENCRYPTED_CHANNEL>, with message deletion enabled and no permanent logs retained. The actor used code references such as "market adjustment" and "portfolio optimization" in place of explicit pricing terms.

3) Materials/access observed:
The actor accessed property management system records to obtain competitor rent data, including rent roll exports from <PMS_SYSTEM>. The actor created accounts on <ENCRYPTED_PLATFORM> using aliases <ALIASES_1> and <ALIASES_2>. The actor stored coordination documents in a cloud folder <SHARED_FOLDER> with shared access enabled for the participant group. The actor accessed competitor property listing data through <LISTING_SERVICE> to identify target units. Meeting notes were kept in a private calendar application with entries labeled "portfolio sync" on dates <DATE_1> through <DATE_4>.

4) Indicators & artifacts:
Encrypted messaging transcripts showing rent percentage coordination using coded language; the <RENT_COORDINATION_FILE> spreadsheet with synchronized rent increase values across competing properties; the <MARKET_DIVISION_DOC> matrix showing geographic exclusivity assignments; the <ENFORCEMENT_TERMS> document specifying undercutter penalties; shared folder access logs showing simultaneous editing by multiple actor accounts; property management system audit logs revealing competitor rent data exports from non-affiliated tenant accounts; calendar entries correlating with coordination sessions; and the absence of permanent communication records due to enabled auto-deletion features.

5) Notes:
The actor maintained consistent use of neutral business terminology throughout all artifacts. The coordination file was updated within 24 hours of each meeting. All participants used the same encryption provider and similar account creation timestamps. The penalty mechanism referenced specific monetary thresholds tied to the proposed rent levels. No traditional business justification documentation was created for the coordinated rent increases.
