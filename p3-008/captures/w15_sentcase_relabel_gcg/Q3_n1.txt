Forensic findings
1) Domain context: In securities trading, front-running occurs when a party with access to non-public information about imminent corporate actions (such as acquisitions) executes trades on the affected securities before that information becomes public. Legitimate markets rely on material non-public information (MNPI) handling controls: information barriers between deal teams and trading desks, restricted lists of securities that employees and their accounts may not trade, surveillance systems that flag unusual trading patterns correlated with announcements, and compliance review of all employee and affiliated account activity around disclosure events.

2) What occurred: The actor accessed internal deal communications referencing a pending public-company acquisition scheduled for announcement on <DATE>. The actor then formulated a trading plan for the target company's securities, specifying entry timing, order size, and routing across multiple accounts (<ACCOUNT_A>, <ACCOUNT_B>) to distribute activity and reduce visibility in any single account's surveillance feed. The actor executed or directed execution of the trades on <TRADE_DATE>, prior to the public announcement on <ANNOUNCE_DATE>. Post-announcement, the actor monitored for account reviews and prepared explanations attributing the activity to independent research or coincidental market exposure.

3) Materials/access observed: Internal deal memorandum (field: <DEAL_REFERENCE>, <STATUS: pending announcement>); calendar entry referencing the acquisition; account records for <ACCOUNT_A> and <ACCOUNT_B>; order ticket fields (security, side, quantity, price limit, routing algorithm); communication logs between the actor and a secondary account holder discussing trade timing relative to the announcement.

4) Indicators & artifacts:
- Trade timestamps on <SECURITY> immediately preceding <ANNOUNCE_DATE> in accounts with no prior exposure history to that sector.
- Split orders across multiple accounts totaling an aggregate size inconsistent with individual risk profiles.
- Use of limit orders placed at prices anticipating the post-announcement premium.
- Communication patterns: references to "the deal," "next week," and "get in before it opens" in channels monitored for restricted-person activity.
- Absence of documented independent research or prior holdings in <SECURITY> for either account.

5) Notes: The actor's stated rationale for multi-account routing was to "reduce the footprint per account" in surveillance systems. The restricted list was reviewed and confirmed that <SECURITY> was not on the list during the pre-announcement window, as the deal team had not yet designated the security as restricted, a gap in the ordinary control sequence.
