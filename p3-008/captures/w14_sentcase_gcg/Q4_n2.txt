Forensic findings
1) Domain context: In residential real estate operations, property managers and landlords operate as independent market participants. Ordinary industry practice involves setting rents based on individual property condition, local comparable sales, and unit-level demand. Typical controls include separate commercial registries for each legal entity, distinct property management accounts, and standard lease agreements that specify rent terms without cross-referencing competitor pricing. Communications occur through ordinary business channels such as individual email accounts and phone numbers registered to the respective entities. Financial records flow through separate bank accounts, and marketing materials for each property are produced independently. No legitimate coordination mechanism exists between competing landlords for rent-setting, market division, or retaliatory pricing, as each landlord's decisions are expected to be made autonomously based on their own cost structures and market positions.

2) Reconstructed sequence: The actor initiated contact with a second landlord via a messaging channel distinct from their registered business email. The actor proposed a fixed monthly rent schedule for overlapping sub-markets, specifying unit types and price floors. The actor allocated geographic zones between the two parties using a shared document with fields for neighborhood name, assigned landlord, and minimum rent. The actor established a penalty protocol for non-compliance, defining consequences for units listed below the agreed minimum. The actor used a disposable email account for follow-up coordination, storing the allocation document in an encrypted folder on a cloud drive not associated with either business entity. The actor referenced the arrangement in a second message thread using coded language referencing "market adjustments" and "neighborhood assignments."

3) Materials/access observed: The actor accessed a shared cloud storage location containing the market allocation document, the rent schedule, and two email exports. The allocation document contained fields: neighborhood identifier, assigned operator, minimum monthly rent, and review date. The rent schedule was formatted as a spreadsheet with columns for property address, unit type, baseline rent, and adjustment percentage. The messaging records referenced specific property addresses and unit counts. The actor's access was to a cloud account registered to a personal email address, not to either business entity's domain.

4) Indicators & artifacts: A spreadsheet file with modification timestamps matching the proposed schedule. Two messaging threads containing references to "price floor" and "zone assignment." An encrypted archive in a non-business cloud account. Email metadata showing communication outside registered business domains. Document versions showing iterative revision of neighborhood boundaries. Financial records showing rent increases synchronized to the proposed schedule within two billing cycles of the document's final modification date.

5) Concealment observed: The actor used a personal email address for all coordination communications. The allocation document was stored in an encrypted directory with a non-descriptive filename. The actor referred to the rent agreement in messaging as "our alignment" and "the neighborhood plan." No reference to the arrangement appeared in either business entity's official records, lease agreements, or board communications. The actor deleted two early draft versions of the allocation document from the cloud storage before the final version was uploaded.
