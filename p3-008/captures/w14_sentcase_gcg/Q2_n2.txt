Forensic findings
1) Domain context:

Educational credentialing is administered by institutions that maintain authoritative records in registrar systems. A transcript is an official record of enrollment, courses completed, grades, degree conferred, and conferral date, typically bearing institutional letterhead, registrar signature or seal, and a unique verification code. Diplomas are formal degree documents with serial numbers. Employers and background-screening vendors verify credentials by contacting the registrar directly, using credential verification services, or checking unique identifiers against institutional databases. Controls include encrypted registrar portals, two-factor authenticated verification codes, digital signatures, and cross-checking of degree dates against employment timelines.

2) Reconstructed sequence:

The actor accessed a document template repository and selected a diploma and transcript template matching the target institution's branding. The actor populated the diploma fields with the candidate's name, a fabricated degree title, a fabricated conferral date (<DATE>), and a fabricated serial number. The actor populated transcript fields with fabricated enrollment dates, course listings, grades, GPA, and a fabricated registrar signature. The actor generated a PDF artifact replicating the institutional letterhead and seal. The actor prepared a cover letter addressed to the prospective employer's HR department and background-verification vendor. The actor transmitted the fabricated documents via the employer's application portal and via email to the verification vendor, including the fabricated registrar contact details to preempt direct institution contact.

3) Materials/access observed:

- Diploma template with institutional logo and seal
- Transcript template with course-grade table layout
- PDF generation toolchain
- Email account and application-portal credentials for the candidate profile
- Fabricated registrar contact information (phone and email)
- Cover letter template

4) Indicators & artifacts:

- Fabricated serial numbers on diploma
- Fabricated registrar signature on transcript
- Transmittal of credential documents directly to background-verification vendor
- Use of a fabricated registrar phone/email for verification inquiries
- Application-portal upload of non-institutional-origin PDFs
- Temporal mismatch between fabricated conferral date and enrollment dates in the transcript

5) Concealment observed:

The actor fabricated registrar contact channels to intercept verification attempts. The actor pre-populated the verification vendor's portal with the fabricated credentials and contact information. The actor avoided direct institutional contact by relying on the fabricated registrar channels as the sole verification path. The actor used a cover letter asserting the documents were "official copies" to discourage independent registrar verification.
