Forensic findings CASEID FILE NOTE
1) Domain context: An institutional records office maintains a controlled environment for the creation, retention, and audit of administrative documents. Standard intake procedures require that each file be assigned a unique identifier, a classification code, and a date of receipt. Documents are indexed by originator, subject, and handling authority, with access restricted to designated custodians. Retention schedules mandate preservation periods based on document category, while periodic audits verify chain-of-custody integrity and detect unauthorized alterations. Access logs record every retrieval, modification, and transmission event, including the account identifier, timestamp, and action type. Discrepancies between source records and filed copies are flagged for review, and any attempt to bypass standard filing channels is logged as an access-control exception. The office maintains a master ledger of active files, a restricted archive for sealed or high-sensitivity records, and a quarantine repository for materials pending adjudication.

2) What occurred: The actor initiated a series of document filings through a standard intake channel, submitting a request form containing a field for donor identification, a field for financial obligation status, and a field for recipient routing. The actor entered a named account into the donor identification field and marked the financial obligation status field with a value indicating severe indebtedness. The actor then filed a consent record, altering the consent field to reflect agreement, while the original source document retained a value indicating refusal. A second set of documents, including medical clearance forms, was filed with dated records that did not correspond to the intake dates. The actor transmitted these documents through a secondary channel, bypassing the primary intake desk, to a foreign routing account. The routing account was listed in the master ledger as inactive. The actor filed a final document in the restricted archive, referencing a set of authority-exemption clauses that were not present in the standard filing index.

3. Materials/access observed: The actor accessed the standard intake channel using a named account with standard custodian permissions. The actor also accessed the secondary transmission channel using an account identifier not listed in the master ledger. The actor retrieved two medical clearance forms from the restricted archive, which were subsequently altered. The actor filed the altered forms back into the restricted archive, overwriting the original dated records. The actor accessed the foreign routing account, which was marked as sealed in the master ledger, and transmitted the altered documents through that account.

4) Indicators & artifacts: Artifacts observed include a request form with a financial obligation status field marked as "severe indebtedness" and a consent field value inconsistent with the source document. A dated medical clearance form was found with an intake date preceding the donor identification date. A transmission log entry showed a document routed through a sealed foreign account. The master ledger contained an entry for the foreign routing account flagged as "inactive," yet access logs recorded three retrieval events from that account. The restricted archive contained two versions of the medical clearance form: one with the original dated records and one with altered values. The altered version bore a filing timestamp consistent with the secondary channel transmission time. No standard intake desk log entries corresponded to the altered documents.
