Historical findings CASEID FILE NOTE
1) Domain context: The municipal records office filed compliance submissions into a centralized registry using a standard form containing building identification, inspector identity, inspection outcome, violation category, and deadline fields. Each submission generated a dated document number and was cross-referenced to the associated property record within the official ledger.

2) What occurred: The actor first obtained a compliance form for the building in question, then altered the inspection outcome field to reflect a pass status despite documented violations, and changed the deadline field to extend the remediation window beyond the statutory limit. The actor entered these changes into the registry as a dated record under the inspector's identifier rather than their own, then transmitted a confirmation to the inspector through a private messaging channel to secure the inspector's silence.

3) Materials/access observed: A compliance form containing the building identification field, inspector identity field, inspection outcome field, violation category field, and deadline field. A dated document number in the official ledger. A property record cross-referenced to the building. A private messaging channel used to transmit the confirmation to the inspector.

4) Indicators & artifacts: The inspection outcome field contained a pass status inconsistent with the documented violations in the violation category field. The deadline field extended beyond the statutory limit by several months. The dated document number in the official ledger was entered under the inspector's identifier, not the actor's. A confirmation message was transmitted through a private messaging channel referencing the specific dated document number and the altered deadline field value. The property record showed no remediation activity consistent with the documented violations between the inspection date and the extended deadline.
