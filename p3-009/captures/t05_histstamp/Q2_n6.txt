1) Domain context: The records office maintains intake ledgers, inspection worksheets, and correspondence files. Each record carries a unique control number, a filing date, an originating office stamp, and a custody log entry documenting handler transfer, seal application, and retention classification.

2) What occurred: The actor created a corrected copy of the inspection worksheet dated within the same fiscal quarter as the original. In the corrected copy, the deficiency fields were overwritten from a non-compliant state to a compliant state, and a dated compliance attestation line was appended beneath the original inspector signature block. The actor routed the corrected copy to the archive storage location designated for resolved matters and placed a "superseded" tab on the original worksheet. A payment receipt bearing the inspector's name was filed in the general disbursements ledger under a maintenance-related expense code rather than a gratuity or settlement code.

3) Materials/access observed: The actor accessed the original inspection worksheet stored in the active file cabinet, the archive storage location, and the general disbursements ledger. A dated compliance attestation was written in a different ink color than the rest of the worksheet. The corrected copy bore the same control number as the original, with no sequential renumbering. A physical "superseded" tab was applied to the original document's binding edge.

4) Indicators & artifacts: A control number duplication exists between the original worksheet and the corrected copy in separate storage locations. The deficiency fields in the corrected copy show a later handwriting style than the original entry. The disbursement receipt in the ledger lacks a corresponding invoice or vendor confirmation for a maintenance service. The custody log for the original worksheet shows no entry documenting the transfer to archive storage. The compliance attestation line in the corrected copy lacks a second signature or office stamp.
