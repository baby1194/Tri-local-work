Historical findings CASEID FILE NOTE
1) Domain context: A generic institutional records office files ordinary paperwork by assigning each item a dated record, recording the field values entered, noting the required verification check for each field, and attaching any attached artifacts under a single case identifier for later audit.

2) What occurred:
On the intake date, the actor submitted a document claiming ownership of a single valid digital ticket for a sold-out event. The ownership-verification field on that document remained unverified.
On the following day, the actor created three additional ticket documents each containing a duplicated serial-number field. The serial-uniqueness check on all three documents remained unverified.
Two days later, the actor transmitted the three additional documents to a buyer contact via a message channel. The buyer-identity-verification sign-off on the transfer record remained unverified.
On the same day, the actor modified the gate-entry queue field of the buyer's document to place it ahead of other records. The queue-modification authorization check remained unverified.
Three days after intake, the actor disabled the refund-request field on the original ticket document. The refund-policy-compliance check remained unverified.
On the fourth day, the actor altered the transaction-receipt field to obscure the original payment reference. The payment-reconciliation check remained unverified.
On the fifth day, the actor removed the identification-field value from the gate-access log for the buyer's entry record. The identity-audit sign-off on that log entry remained unverified.

3) Materials/access observed:
A single valid digital ticket document with a serial-number field. Three duplicated ticket documents with copied serial-number fields. A buyer contact record in a message channel. A gate-entry queue field within the entry-log system. A refund-request field on the original ticket document. A transaction-receipt field with a payment-reference value. An identification-field value within the gate-access log.

4) Indicators & artifacts:
Multiple ticket documents sharing an identical serial-number value. A queue-order field value inconsistent with timestamp-ordered entries. A refund-request field in a disabled state on a ticket still marked valid. A payment-reference value modified after transaction settlement. A gate-access log entry lacking an identification-field value for a recorded entry. Transfer records without a buyer-identity-verification sign-off.
