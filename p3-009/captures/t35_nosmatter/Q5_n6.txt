Historical findings CASEID FILE NOTE
1) Domain context: A generic institutional records office files ordinary paperwork by logging document types, standard field values, transaction channels, and account references, preserving chronological order, unique identifiers, and access metadata in structured registers without annotating intent or legality.
2) What occurred: The actor created a dated record duplicating a single valid digital ticket, generating several copies with altered ticket identifiers. The actor transmitted the duplicated documents through a secondary channel to a buyer account, specifying a sequence in which the buyer’s document was processed at the gate before the original. The actor modified refund and chargeback fields in the account ledger to suppress reversal transactions. The actor removed or masked identification fields associated with the original account in subsequent records.
3) Materials/access observed: The actor accessed the original digital ticket document, including its unique identifier, dated field, and embedded account reference. The actor accessed the ticketing platform’s issuance channel to generate additional documents. The actor accessed the buyer’s account record and gate-processing queue field to alter validation order. The actor accessed the financial ledger’s refund, chargeback, and identification-linking fields.
4) Indicators & artifacts: The register shows multiple ticket documents sharing the same source identifier with divergent secondary fields. Timestamps indicate rapid sequential issuance from one account. The gate log records a buyer document validated prior to the original, despite the original’s earlier creation date. Ledger entries show nullified refund and chargeback flags on the associated transaction. Account records display deleted or blanked identification fields following the ticket duplication event.
