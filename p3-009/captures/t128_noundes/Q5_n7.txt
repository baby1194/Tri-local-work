Historical findings CASEID-0142 FILE NOTE

1) Domain context:
The institution files ordinary requests through a single shared ledger. Each entry records the actor identifier, transaction timestamp, document type, and verification status. Standard procedure requires cross-referencing the request against the source ledger and obtaining two independent verification signatures before the record is marked complete.

2) What occurred:
On the date in question, the actor created a document in the sales ledger duplicating the identifier of an existing single-item entry, and the system's uniqueness check for that identifier field was not triggered.
The actor then submitted a secondary ledger entry referencing the same item, and the verification signature required for that field was not obtained.
Subsequently, the actor routed the new document to the access-control channel, and the manual gate-verification check for that entry was not performed.
The actor also modified the refund-eligibility field of the original document, and the supervisor review for that field remained pending.
Finally, the actor updated the payment method field to obscure the transaction origin, and the identity-verification step for that account was not completed.

3) Materials/access observed:
The actor used a named account with administrator-level write access to the sales ledger.
The actor accessed the raw item database via a privileged API channel.
The actor had read access to the verification-signature queue, where pending status was visible.

4) Indicators & artifacts:
A duplicate ledger entry with an identical item identifier.
A missing verification signature on the secondary entry.
An access-control record with a pending gate check.
A refund-eligibility field change without supervisor approval.
A payment-method field change linked to an unverified account.
