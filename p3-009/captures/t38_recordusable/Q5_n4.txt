Historical findings CASEID FILE NOTE
1) Domain context: A generic institutional records office maintains ordinary administrative paperwork through standardized intake documents, versioned field values, and dated account entries, preserving document identifiers, access channels, and chronological sequences for later audit and reconciliation.

2) What occurred: The actor created a document derived from a named ticket record and copied its identifying fields into multiple dated record instances. The actor modified the field values in those instances to present distinct identifiers while retaining reference to the same named event. The actor routed copies through a named digital channel and a named payment channel, pairing each copy with an account entry dated on the intake day. The actor set a sequence field so that the first record carried an earlier timestamp than the others. The actor disabled the refund field and the chargeback flag on each account entry and marked the identification field as cleared.

3) Materials/access observed: The actor accessed the named ticket record containing the event identifier, the seat field, and the barcode field. The actor used a named digital wallet account, a named payment processor channel, and a named messaging channel to transmit the copies. The actor read the gate-scan field format and the ticket-verification endpoint documentation. No additional account credentials beyond the named wallet were observed.

4) Indicators & artifacts: Multiple dated records sharing the same event identifier but carrying distinct serial fields. A refund field set to zero across all account entries. A chargeback flag set to false. A sequence field ordering one record ahead of the others. Identification fields marked cleared without a corresponding verification document. Duplicate transmissions of the barcode field across three or more channel messages within a short window.
