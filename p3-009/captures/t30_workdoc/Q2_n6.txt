Historical findings CASEID FILE NOTE
1) Domain context: A records office files incoming paperwork into a dated ledger, assigns a reference number to each document, and stores the original together with a scanned copy in an archive index. Routine intake includes a signature field, a date field, and a status field.

2) What occurred: The actor produced a document titled Inspection Report with fields for inspector name, date, and outcome. The actor set the outcome field to Pass, left the inspector name field as a generic descriptor, and submitted it via a channel marked as official. The actor produced a second document, a payment receipt, with fields for amount and recipient name, set the recipient to the inspector's name, and filed it in the same ledger. The actor altered the date field on the first document to an earlier date, and added a note field containing a phrase indicating the report was final.

3) Materials/access observed: The actor had access to the ledger system, the scanner, and the archive storage. The documents were produced on standard paper stock. The channels used were the official submission portal and a private messaging channel between the actor and the inspector's account.

4) Indicators & artifacts: The date field on the report was inconsistent with the timestamp on the scanned copy. The payment receipt matched no account in the ledger. The note field was added after the initial filing. The private channel log showed two messages dated before the report was filed.
