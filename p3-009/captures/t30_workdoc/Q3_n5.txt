Historical findings CASEID FILE NOTE
1) Domain context:
The records office files routine paperwork through standardized intake documents, assigns sequential control numbers, routes forms through designated approval channels, and archives completed manifests in dated, indexed storage with access logs maintained per role.

2) What occurred:
The actor drafted a working document in a personal directory, dated to a specific business day, containing a routing table. The actor entered a field for a non-licensed receiving entity, a field for an unscheduled delivery date, and a field for a site identifier not present in the approved processor registry. The actor duplicated a manifest form from a prior quarter, altered the waste classification field to a non-hazardous code, and modified the net weight field to a lower value than the inventory ledger. The actor entered a manifest control number that did not follow the sequential numbering pattern of the prior 12 months. The actor transmitted a copy of the modified manifest through an unmonitored file-share channel to a named account associated with a third-party broker, outside the approved electronic filing system.

3) Materials/access observed:
The working document resided in a personal file system with no version control. The manifest form carried a header referencing a prior-year template. The modified classification field corresponded to a code for general industrial waste. The net weight field showed a value inconsistent with the scale tickets in the inventory ledger for the same period. The file-share channel was a consumer-grade service without an enterprise access log. The named account belonged to an external party not listed in the approved vendor master file.

4) Indicators & artifacts:
The manifest control number broke the sequential pattern. The waste classification field did not match the material safety data sheet on file for the product in question. The net weight discrepancy exceeded the 5% tolerance documented in the internal audit procedure. The delivery date field fell on a weekend. The file-share channel had no corresponding entry in the electronic filing system's transmission log. The receiving entity field matched no entry in the licensed processor registry. The working document contained no approval signature or reviewer stamp.
