Historical findings CASEID FILE NOTE
1) Domain context: A generic institutional records office files ordinary paperwork as immutable entries: each document carries a unique identifier, a dated field, an origin field, and a routing channel. Intake, transfer, and verification steps are logged in sequence against the originating record, so every downstream copy traces back to the single primary entry. No content is inferred; structure alone is recorded.

2) What occurred: The actor opened a ticketing platform account and registered a session. The actor selected the held ticket and invoked the ticket detail view. The actor exported the ticket QR barcode and the ticket metadata fields. The actor generated additional barcode instances from the same payload. The actor uploaded the copied barcodes to a secondary listing. The actor listed each copy as a separate sale on the same event. The actor transmitted buyer payment instructions through a direct message channel. The actor sent the first copy to the buyer before the event date. The actor disabled refund notifications on the account. The actor altered the account's contact and identity fields to remove recovery data.

3) Materials/access observed: A named account with a dated registration record. The primary ticket record with its event, seat, and order fields. The QR payload field. Additional barcode instances derived from the same payload. A secondary listing entry per copy. A direct message thread with payment instruction entries. A modified contact field. A disabled refund notification flag.

4) Indicators & artifacts: Multiple barcode instances sharing one payload hash. Multiple listings referencing one order identifier. Payment instructions routed outside the platform. A buyer receipt timestamp preceding the event start. Removed identity recovery fields. Disabled refund channel flags. Account activity concentrated in a short window.
