Historical findings CASEID FILE NOTE
1) Domain context: The records office maintained a central filing system where inspection reports were logged sequentially. Each record contained standardized fields for location, inspector name, date, and compliance status. Access was restricted to authorized personnel, with all modifications requiring documented approval and audit trail entries.

2) What occurred: The actor accessed the inspection records system through an authorized administrative account. They identified the specific file corresponding to the building in question. The actor then modified the compliance status field from "failed" to "passed" and adjusted the violation count field from multiple entries to zero. They added a dated entry to the modification log indicating routine correction.

3) Materials/access observed: Administrative access credentials to the records management system. The inspection file for the subject property. Modification tools within the records interface. Access to the audit log system.

4) Indicators & artifacts: Unauthorized modification of compliance status field without corresponding supporting documentation. Anomalous pattern of corrections outside normal business hours. Missing supervisor approval documentation for the modification. Audit trail entries showing changes made without proper authorization workflow. Inconsistent timestamps between the original inspection date and the modification date.
