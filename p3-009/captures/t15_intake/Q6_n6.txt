Historical findings CASEID FILE NOTE
1) Domain context:

Generic institutional records offices maintain chronological filing systems where documents are indexed by date, account, and classification. Ordinary paperwork is logged through standardized intake channels, assigned reference numbers, and stored in bound registers or digital repositories. Access to these systems is role-based, with all modifications requiring dual authorization and timestamped audit trails.

2) What occurred:

The actor accessed the maintenance log register for a named aircraft account. A dated record was located in the component history file. The actor modified the part serial number field to match a previously approved part identifier. The installation date field was altered to a prior date within the scheduled maintenance window. A second entry in the airworthiness certificate log was amended to show continuous compliance. The changes were submitted through the standard maintenance management system interface. A notification was sent via the internal email channel to the quality assurance account. A revised technical log extract was printed and placed in the aircraft technical file folder.

3) Materials/access observed:

The actor used a named user account with maintenance planner privileges. Access was to the digital maintenance management system, the paper-bound aircraft technical log, and the internal email server. The specific documents accessed were the component history file, the airworthiness certificate register, and the scheduled maintenance plan. The fields modified included part identifiers, installation dates, and compliance status flags.

4) Indicators & artifacts:

The digital audit trail showed the named account accessing the component history file outside scheduled maintenance periods. The part serial number field contained a value matching a part from a different aircraft account. The installation date was inconsistent with the weather report archive for that period. The email notification lacked the required quality assurance code field. The printed technical log extract bore the actor's initials instead of the designated inspector's signature. The airworthiness certificate register showed a gap in the sequential numbering of compliance stamps.
