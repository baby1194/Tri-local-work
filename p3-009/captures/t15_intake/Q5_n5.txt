Historical findings CASEID FILE NOTE
1) Domain context: A generic institutional records office files ordinary paperwork as versioned documents with controlled fields, unique identifiers, and audited channels. Each record carries an owner, a status, and a timestamp, and modifications require authorized access and leave a trace in the access log.
2) What occurred: The actor opened a valid ticket document, copied its identifier field and event metadata, and generated multiple duplicate documents with sequential serial fields. The actor transmitted the copies through a public marketplace channel, each listing referencing the same event date and seat allocation. The actor forwarded one copy via a private message channel to a buyer account, attaching instructions to present it at the gate. The actor disabled the refund field on the original document and altered the chargeback status field to a null state, and removed the identity verification flag from the account profile.
3) Materials/access observed: A valid ticket document with an issuer signature field, a marketplace listing channel, a private messaging channel, a gate verification interface, and the account holder's identity verification record.
4) Indicators & artifacts: Multiple ticket documents sharing one issuer signature but carrying distinct serial fields; a single seat allocation referenced in more than one document; a listing channel message referencing a gate entry that preceded the seller's own gate entry; a refund field set to disabled on the source document; a chargeback status field altered to null; and an account profile with the identity verification flag removed after the transaction.
