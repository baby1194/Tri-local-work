Historical findings CASEID FILE NOTE
1) Domain context: Municipal building code divisions maintain digital inspection ledgers, paper correspondence folders, and payment portals. Officers log visit outcomes, assign violation codes, and issue notices. Finance systems process remediation invoices and mark cases as closed when balances reach zero.

2) What occurred: The actor accessed the inspection case file corresponding to a dated property address. The actor modified the violation status field from an active code to a resolved code and altered the inspector signature field to reflect a fictional officer. The actor created a fabricated receipt document referencing a payment channel and a specific transaction identifier. The actor uploaded this document to the case file's attachment field. The actor sent a message via a personal channel to a second account, referencing the altered status and requesting the original paper ledger be discarded. The actor submitted a falsified certificate of correction through the public portal, citing the fabricated transaction identifier.

3) Materials/access observed: A digital inspection ledger entry with a modified timestamp. A PDF receipt bearing a generated QR code and a non-standard vendor name. An email thread in a personal account discussing the "removal" of a physical folder. A public portal submission log showing an IP address outside the municipality's operational range. A handwritten note in the discarded paper folder matching the actor's penmanship.

4) Indicators & artifacts: Discrepancy between the digital status change timestamp and the payment portal's zero-activity logs. Use of a generic email address to submit the certificate of correction. The physical paper ledger for that quarter being missing from the archival bin. Metadata on the uploaded receipt showing creation by a document generator rather than a payment terminal. The second account being registered under a name similar to, but not identical to, the assigned inspector.
