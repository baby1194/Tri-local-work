1) Domain context: A records office receives intake forms on standard forms, assigns a reference number, enters data into named fields, routes documents through defined channels, and stores completed files in the archive.

2) What occurred: The actor completed an intake form (reference number: a numeric identifier) describing a component swap on a passenger aircraft. The actor prepared two maintenance-log entries dated within a defined range, each containing a part-serial-number field, an installation date, a technician signature field, and an approval-stamp field, and entered the new component's serial number into the part-serial-number field of an existing log line. The actor amended the part-traceability record: the original supplier field was overwritten with a second supplier's identifier, and the date-of-arrival field was shifted by a defined number of days. The actor created a new airworthiness-approval request, populated the aircraft registration field, the component description field, and an "exception" text field with a narrative describing the change as a scheduled replacement, and submitted it through the regulator's electronic submission portal (channel identifier: an alphanumeric code). The actor directed a colleague to countersign the approval request using a shared credentials account. The actor attached a compliance certificate bearing a certification number and filed all documents under the aircraft's tail number in the maintenance records database.

3) Materials/access observed: Maintenance-log form (fields: part serial number, installation date, technician signature, approval stamp); part-traceability record (fields: supplier identifier, date of arrival); airworthiness-approval request form (fields: aircraft registration, component description, exception narrative); electronic submission portal account (shared credentials account); compliance certificate (certification number field); maintenance records database (indexed by tail number).

4) Indicators & artifacts: A maintenance-log line containing a part serial number inconsistent with the part-traceability record; a date-of-arrival field shifted relative to supplier shipping records; an "exception" narrative describing an unscheduled replacement as a scheduled one; a shared credentials account used for countersignature; a certification number present on the compliance certificate but absent from the issuing authority's registry; and a file folder under the tail number containing documents with out-of-sequence reference numbers.
