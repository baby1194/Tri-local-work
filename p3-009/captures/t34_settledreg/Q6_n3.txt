Historical findings CASEID FILE NOTE
1) Domain context: A generic institutional records office files ordinary paperwork as dated entries with named fields (item id, date, entry text, entry account). Corrections are appended as new dated entries that reference the prior entry. Channels are logged as source systems, and access is recorded by account name and timestamp.

2) What occurred: The actor created an undated entry in the aircraft maintenance record describing the replacement component with a generic part identifier and a completion date matching the prior service entry. The actor entered a second record in the parts history document with a serial number field, a supplier field, and an airworthiness-certificate reference field, all populated with values consistent with the original component. The actor submitted both documents through the maintenance tracking system on an account matching the assigned maintenance engineer, with a submission timestamp preceding the scheduled inspection date.

3) Materials/access observed: Aircraft maintenance record document (entry fields: item id, date, entry text, entry account). Parts history document (fields: serial number, supplier, airworthiness-certificate reference, date). Maintenance tracking system (submission channel, account name, timestamp field). Scheduled inspection date (reference value).

4) Indicators & artifacts: Entry text in the maintenance record lacking a corresponding parts-receipt document. Serial number field in the parts history document with no matching supplier invoice record. Airworthiness-certificate reference field with no matching certificate-issuance record. Submission timestamp on the maintenance tracking system preceding the scheduled inspection date. Account name on the submission matching the assigned maintenance engineer, with no entry in the parts-receipt log for the replacement component.
