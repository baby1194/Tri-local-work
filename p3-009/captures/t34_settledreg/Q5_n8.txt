1) Domain context: An institutional records office files ordinary paperwork by assigning a unique identifier, timestamping each event, cataloguing source documents, and storing them in a controlled ledger with access logs, preserving the original format and metadata for audit review.
2) What occurred: The actor created duplicate document records referencing the same serial field and event date. The actor recorded a sequence field indicating priority for the first recipient record. The actor filed records with a retention flag set to suppress reversal processing and an identifier field marked for non-correlation with prior ledger entries.
3) Materials/access observed: One master record with a unique serial field, a digital transfer channel, a recipient field for each duplicate record, a sequence field, a retention flag, and an identifier suppression field. The actor had write access to the record ledger and the transfer channel interface.
4) Indicators & artifacts: Duplicate serial fields across multiple records, identical origin fields with distinct recipient fields, a sequence field value indicating priority, a retention flag suppressing reversal, an identifier field marked for non-correlation, and access log entries showing rapid sequential filing of duplicates.
