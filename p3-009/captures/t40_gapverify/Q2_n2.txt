Historical findings CASEID FILE NOTE
1) Domain context: An institutional records office files inspection reports as dated documents containing violation codes, remediation deadlines, and official sign-off fields. Each document is assigned a unique identifier, indexed by address, and archived to a controlled repository pending closure.

2) What occurred: On a specified date, the actor submitted a dated remediation payment record to the building account, leaving the payment confirmation field unverified. The actor then altered the violation status field in the inspection document from "open" to "closed," with the inspector sign-off field left unverified. Next, the actor appended a falsified compliance certificate to the case file, where the certificate authenticity field remained unverified. The actor finally transmitted the modified file via an unmonitored communication channel, with the recipient acknowledgment field unverified.

3) Materials/access observed: The actor accessed the central inspection database using a named account. The actor viewed the original inspection document and the associated payment ledger. The actor used a word processor to create a duplicate certificate with a matching document identifier.

4) Indicators & artifacts: The payment record lacks a transaction reference number. The status change timestamp postdates the original inspection by one day, with no corresponding entry in the change log. The certificate carries a sequential identifier that does not appear in the certificate issuance register. The communication channel log shows a message sent outside standard business hours without a case tracking ID.
