Historical findings CASEID FILE NOTE
1) Domain context: The records office files standard inspection reports, payment receipts, and correspondence into bound volumes. Each document bears a document ID, an entry date, and an examiner signature. Transfers between offices travel through logged courier channels with seal identifiers, and all files carry cross-reference stamps for audit retrieval.

2) What occurred: The actor prepared a revised inspection report document, replacing the field "violation list" with a field reading "no violations found." The actor altered the field "examiner signature" to bear a named inspector's name and a dated record. The actor submitted a payment receipt document referencing a sum of money and a named recipient account. The actor filed both documents under the original document ID, overwriting the prior entry, and routed the package through the standard courier channel with a fresh seal identifier.

3) Materials/access observed: The actor accessed the inspection file volume, the payment register, and the courier intake log. The actor used a named administrative account to modify the report document and to register the receipt. The actor obtained the inspector's signature block from a prior dated record and reproduced it on the revised document.

4) Indicators & artifacts: The document ID bears a later entry date than the original filed date. The examiner signature field differs in ink characteristics from prior entries. The payment receipt references an account with no matching vendor record. The courier seal identifier on the resubmitted package does not match the intake log for that date. The violation list field is blank where a prior version recorded line items.
