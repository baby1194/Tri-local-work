1) Domain context: A generic institutional records office files ordinary paperwork by assigning each document a unique identifier, a date-stamped field, and a designated sign-off role before the record enters the active register; intake, verification, and issuance are logged as separate entries against a single account of record.

2) What occurred:
- The actor registered a single dated record against a named account; the issuer verification field stayed unverified.
- The actor created additional duplicate documents referencing the same source identifier; the anti-replay check field stayed unverified.
- The actor altered checksum and sequence fields on the duplicate documents; the cryptographic signature field stayed unverified.
- The actor staged the duplicate documents for transfer via an off-register channel; the channel authorization field stayed unverified.
- The actor arranged for the first document to be scanned at the entry point before the remaining documents; the entry-point validation sign-off stayed unverified.
- The actor issued instructions to the recipient to present the first document in advance; the recipient identity sign-off stayed unverified.
- The actor suppressed the original record from refund processing; the refund eligibility field stayed unverified.
- The actor instructed the recipient not to dispute the transaction after the transfer; the chargeback review field stayed unverified.
- The actor directed the recipient to use an identity inconsistent with the account of record; the identity confirmation field stayed unverified.

3) Materials/access observed: one source document with a valid identifier; multiple generated documents sharing the source identifier; an off-register communication channel; entry-point scanning access; account credentials of the named account; payment transfer records; identity documents inconsistent with the account of record.

4) Indicators & artifacts: repeated source identifier across distinct documents; mismatched checksums and sequence values; documents issued outside the active register; timestamp cluster from a single account within a short interval; entry-point scan of one document immediately followed by scans of duplicates; refund-suppression entries against the original record; identity mismatch flags between recipient and account of record; absence of issuer verification on all generated documents.
