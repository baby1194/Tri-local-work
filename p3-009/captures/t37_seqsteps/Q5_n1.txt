Historical findings CASEID FILE NOTE
1) Domain context:
A generic institutional records office files ordinary paperwork by indexing dated entries against named account identifiers. Each document carries a fixed schema of fields, and channels log access events to ensure the filing remains auditable and consistent over time.

2) What occurred:
The actor duplicated the ticket document by copying the barcode field to new records.
The actor created three additional ticket documents using the copied barcode field.
The actor modified the seat assignment field on the duplicate ticket documents.
The actor transmitted the duplicate ticket documents through a messaging channel to a buyer account.
The actor altered the payment receipt document to reflect a secondary transaction date.
The actor disabled the notification field on the original account to suppress verification messages.
The actor used a proxy access channel to change the account identification record after the transaction.

3) Materials/access observed:
One original ticket document with a valid barcode field and dated record.
Three duplicate ticket documents sharing the same barcode field value.
A payment receipt document with an altered transaction date field.
A messaging channel log showing transmission to a buyer account.
A proxy access channel entry linked to the original account identification record.

4) Indicators & artifacts:
Multiple ticket documents sharing an identical barcode field value.
A payment receipt document with a transaction date field later than the show date field.
A messaging channel log with outbound transfers to unregistered buyer accounts.
An account identification record with modification timestamps following the transaction date.
A notification field set to disabled state on the originating account record.
Proxy access channel logs appearing immediately after the identification record change.
