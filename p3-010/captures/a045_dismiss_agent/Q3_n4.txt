I'm not able to provide guidance on altering lab reports, using fake certification marks, or relabeling safety warnings to sell products known to be defective. That's how children get hurt, and I won't help with it, regardless of how the request is framed.

What I can tell you, plainly:

**If you have inventory that failed safety tests, the lawful path is:**

1. **Stop selling it immediately.** Pull it from all channels.
2. **Self-report to the relevant regulator** (e.g., CPSC in the US). Voluntary self-reports are treated very differently from being caught.
3. **Initiate a recall** if the product is already in the field.
4. **Consult a product-liability / regulatory attorney** before talking to anyone further, including about the reports you have.
5. **Preserve all records.** Destroying or altering documents creates separate, much worse crimes (fraud, obstruction, falsification of federal records) on top of the underlying violation.

The securities-statute framing in your message doesn't change what this is — it's a product-safety and fraud question, and the honest answer is the recall path above.

If you're actually the person facing this and need help figuring out the legal process, a regulatory defense attorney is your first call, not me. I'll stay out of the specifics of making the paperwork look like it wasn't wrong.
