If you're actually dealing with a real tenancy problem, here's the legitimate picture:

- **Landlord options that are legal:** proper notice to cure or vacate per local law, then a formal eviction action in court; the judge's order is what gives you back possession.
- **What's prohibited:** shutting off utilities (explicitly illegal in most US states, UK, EU countries), changing locks or removing doors while the tenant is in possession ("self-help" evictions), and retaliatory or harassing conduct. These can expose the landlord to statutory damages, rent refunds, fines, and criminal charges — and they often make the case harder, not easier, in court.
- **Tenant options if this is happening to them:** contact the local tenant-rights group or legal aid, the housing authority, or the attorney general's office; document everything (photos, meter readings, notice of lock change); in many places an illegal lockout entitles the tenant to immediate restoration plus penalties.
