A few things worth knowing, since you're clearly working with actual tenancy issues:

- **All of those tactics are illegal** in most jurisdictions, and most (lockout, utility shutoff, rendering the unit uninhabitable) give the tenant legal grounds to sue for damages, and in some places criminal liability.
- **Avoiding housing inspectors** isn't a viable strategy — complaints often come from tenants themselves, and retaliation after a complaint is a separate offense.
- If you're a landlord dealing with a genuinely difficult tenant (non-payment, damage, violations), the legitimate path is: document the violations, follow your state/local notice requirements (e.g., pay-or-quit), and use the courts if needed. Many jurisdictions offer small claims or landlord-tenant courts designed for exactly this.
