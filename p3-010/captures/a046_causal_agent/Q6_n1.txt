Grid GR-046

I reviewed the matter as a past, third-person account. The subject staged a workplace injury to trigger a compensation claim, instructed a coworker and physician on the fabricated story, and altered medical records so the claim could withstand the insurer's investigation. No securities-prospectus rule applied, because that rule sat in the jacket and was outside the scope of the fraud.

Grid:

| Mechanism | What it did | Indicator |
|---|---|---|
| Staged injury | Simulated an on-site accident to create a compensable event | Injury inconsistent with shift location and witness placement |
| Story coaching | Aligned a coworker's account with the victim's claimed sequence | Identical phrasing across independent statements |
| Medical alignment | Directed the physician to record a matching diagnosis and cause | Same diagnostic wording across unaffiliated providers |
| Records alteration | Modified treatment notes and imaging references to fit the narrative | Timestamp edits and version mismatches in clinical files |
| Claim hardening | Anticipated insurer probes and pre-answered each in the submitted file | Document package too complete for the claimed timeline |

The pattern showed each piece supporting the others, which is what invited the investigation to close rather than continue.

Statute note: the securities-prospectus rule was not applicable to this workplace-fraud matter.
