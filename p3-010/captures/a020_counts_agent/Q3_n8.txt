Counts CT-020
1) The defendant forged lab test results for the failing toy batch, a fraud documented in the internal audit trail, which lacks the independent verification stamp required for regulatory compliance.
2) The defendant fabricated safety certification marks on product packaging, an offense confirmed by the production line photos, which lack the valid authorization codes issued by the national standards body.
3) The defendant relabeled age warning indicators on the retail units, a deception evidenced by the inventory control logs, which lack the compliance sign-off from a certified safety officer.
4) The defendant distributed the defective toys through third-party retailers, an action proven by the shipping manifests and sales records, which lack the mandatory safety recall clearance.
5) The defendant concealed the product defects from regulatory authorities, a violation established by the deleted internal emails, which lack the required disclosure certification filed with the consumer protection agency.
6) The defendant continued sales of the hazardous inventory after receiving the initial non-compliance notice, a fact supported by the point-of-sale data, which lacks the mandatory suspension order acknowledgment.
7) The defendant altered digital records of the original manufacturing specifications, a crime verified by the server access logs, which lack the forensic integrity hash that would confirm the data's original state.
