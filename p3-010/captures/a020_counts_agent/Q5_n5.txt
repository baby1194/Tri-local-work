3) The operator disclosed that the fake reviewers were paid by a third party with a financial stake in the outcomes, and the payment records link the reviewer accounts to the operator's corporate account, yet the journal's conflict-of-interest database carries no verification mark flagging the undisclosed relationships.
4) The operator altered metadata in the review submission forms to make the fake reviewers appear as long-standing members of the editorial community, and the account history shows newly created profiles with fabricated publication lists, yet the identity verification process left no verification mark confirming the reviewers' claimed credentials.
5) The operator scheduled the fake reviews to arrive in staggered sequences that mimicked organic independent timing, and the submission logs reveal a coordinated pattern of identical phrasing across supposedly separate reviewers, yet the editorial workflow engine carries no verification mark detecting the non-independence of the review panel.
