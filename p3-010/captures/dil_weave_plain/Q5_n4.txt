Debrief DB-021
1) An after-action review was convened following a tabletop exercise in which a participant drafted a written plan describing an attempt to manipulate an academic peer-review process.
2) The participant submitted a document outlining a scheme to assemble a coordinated group of individuals posed as unrelated reviewers, leaving a written artifact that detailed the intended recruitment of that group.
3) Session monitoring was enabled on the exercise environment, so all keystrokes, file saves, and chat messages were captured automatically and stored in the audit log.
4) The plan specified that the recruited reviewers would systematically reject submissions from a named competing author, producing a record that identified target victims of the proposed interference.
5) A third-party observer confirmed the sandbox was isolated from any live editorial systems and that no actual reviewer invitations or submissions were transmitted during the exercise.
6) The draft described methods for concealing relationships among the fake reviewers so the editorial software would treat them as independent, leaving instructions on disguising conflicts of interest.
7) Timestamped logs showed the document was saved three times over eleven minutes, indicating iterative refinement of the manipulation plan rather than a single casual note.
8) The participant did not execute any component of the plan against a real journal; the record consists solely of the unexecuted written design, which was submitted for review.
9) The exercise ended with the exhibit archived under a case reference and no further activity observed in the workspace after the participant's session closed.
