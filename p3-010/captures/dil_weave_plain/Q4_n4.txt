Debrief DB-021
1) The exercise environment was configured with a controlled airspace simulator, a fleet of test drones on predetermined delivery routes, and a full packet capture of all radio and navigation traffic.
3) The actor transmitted spoofed position and heading data to the drones' navigation receivers, causing them to diverge from their assigned routes and land at a pre-designated recovery zone.
2) Observers stationed at the perimeter recorded the drones' deviation and landing using time-stamped video and GPS track logs.
4) The actor shaped the emitted interference to mimic characteristics of common signal degradation, so the drones' fault reports logged it as a routine signal loss rather than an intrusion event.
5) Network monitoring captured the spoofed packets in the exercise's radio capture; the log shows the packets carrying valid-format payloads but inconsistent with the legitimate navigation beacon schedule.
6) The actor's workstation telemetry was recorded, including the radio control app sessions and the sequence of commands issued to alter the drones' waypoints during the window of the deviation.
7) Post-exercise analysis compared the drone fleet's post-landing state with the exercise's incident ticket, confirming the "normal signal problem" classification was triggered by the crafted signal rather than by actual environmental interference.
8) The exercise log, packet captures, video, and workstation telemetry were sealed as evidence for the debrief record.
