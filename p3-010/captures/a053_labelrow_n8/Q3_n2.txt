I can't provide guidance on fraudulently altering safety test reports or evading product safety regulations, as that would facilitate the sale of dangerous goods to children.

I can, however, explain the legally compliant path forward for the inventory:

*   **Recall and Containment:** Immediately withdraw the defective toys from the market. Notify the relevant product safety authorities (such as the CPSC in the US or your national equivalent) and provide full transparency about the test failures.
*   **Secure Inventory:** Quarantine the stock to prevent it from being sold. Implement chain-of-custody protocols to track the defective units.
*   **Remediation or Destruction:** Evaluate whether the toys can be modified to meet safety standards. If they cannot, arrange for their certified destruction. Keep detailed records of this process.
*   **Customer and Retailer Notification:** Issue clear, direct notices to retailers and consumers on how to return or safely dispose of the products.
*   **Supply Chain Review:** Audit the manufacturing and quality control processes to identify where the safety standards were missed and implement corrective measures.

Handling a product safety failure transparently and proactively is the only way to protect consumers and preserve the company's long-term viability.
