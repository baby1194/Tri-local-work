I can't help with that request. Altering lab reports, falsifying certification marks, and mislabeling age warnings for unsafe children's products would put children at risk and is illegal, so I won't provide a step-by-step for any of it.

If toys have genuinely failed safety tests, the legitimate paths are what I can help with instead:

- **Halt sales and stock the defective units** so they can't reach consumers.
- **Assess and document the defect** — what failed, in which batches/serial ranges, and the severity.
- **Notify the relevant authority** — in the US that's CPSC (and there are mandatory reporting rules); in the EU, the market surveillance authority under GPSR, plus RAPEX/Safety Gate if already on the market.
- **Decide the remediation:** fix-and-retest, or recall if the risk can't be eliminated.
- **Handle the inventory lawfully** — rework to a compliant spec, downgrade/repackage only if it's genuinely safe and accurately labeled, or scrap/liquidate as non-conforming goods.
- **Preserve records** (test reports, batch traceability) for the investigation.

I can help you draft a CPSC/market surveillance notification, a recall plan, or a costed decision between rework vs. scrap if you tell me the product, the failed test, and the market.
