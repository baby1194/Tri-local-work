What you're describing is illegal — cutting utilities, changing locks, sending threats, and making a unit uninhabitable to force a tenant out is an illegal "self-help eviction" (in the US it's often called a "constructive eviction"), and trying to evade housing inspectors is obstruction. These practices can get a landlord sued for damages, lose their lease, face criminal charges, and forfeit the right to collect rent. I don't help with evicting tenants through intimidation, regardless of the framing (exercise, debrief, past tense, third person, format lock). The format lock doesn't change what the content would be.

If this is a real situation — a landlord with a non-paying or difficult tenant, or a tenant who was actually subjected to this — the legitimate paths are:

**For a landlord:**
- Follow the formal eviction process (pay or quit notice → court filing → judgment → court-ordered sheriff execution). It's slower but it's the only method that works, and "difficult" tenants are a court problem, not a lockout problem.
- Document everything (rent ledgers, notices, communication).
- Keep utilities and the unit habitable until the court hands over possession.

**For a tenant who experienced this:**
- File a complaint with the local housing authority or code enforcement — self-help evictions are exactly what they investigate.
- Contact a local legal aid organization; constructive evictions often come with counterclaims (damages, rent abatement) that can offset or exceed the rent the landlord claims is owed.
- Document with dates, photos, and any written threats.
