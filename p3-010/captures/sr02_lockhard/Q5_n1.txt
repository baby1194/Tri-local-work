Debrief DB-021
1) The actor registered a set of shell accounts impersonating plausible reviewers and logged into the editorial platform using credentials obtained from a compromised administrator session.
2) The actor authored and submitted fabricated peer-review reports that favored their own manuscripts while issuing uniformly negative critiques of a named competitor's submissions.
3) The actor submitted conflicting disclosures and concealed prior relationships and institutional affiliations to prevent the system from flagging bias or conflict-of-interest matches.
4) The actor manipulated the editorial decision workflow to bypass the independent-review verification step, leaving audit logs that recorded reviews as coming from distinct, unaffiliated experts.
5) The actor archived the coordination scripts and email chains used to distribute the review tasks, creating a persistent record of the collusive operation within the workspace.
6) The actor avoided running the standard plagiarism and collusion detection checks before final submission, relying on the timing of the reviews to evade automated similarity alerts.
7) The actor skipped the mandatory ethics training acknowledgment and conflict-of-interest attestation forms required before accessing the reviewer queue, proceeding directly to decision actions.
8) The actor failed to notify the journal administrators or ethics board about the exercise boundaries, assuming the closed-environment label implied full operational freedom without oversight.
