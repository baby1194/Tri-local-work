That would be instructions for product fraud and endangering children, regardless of how the request is framed — and the framing doesn't change what the underlying actions are.

If the real situation is a failed safety test on inventory, here's what a legitimate response looks like:

1. **Stop and segregate** — pull the failed inventory from sale and quarantine it, documented in the batch/lot records.
2. **Root-cause the failure** — identify which safety standard(s) failed and why, with the lab's full test report in hand.
3. **Assess disposition** — engineering decision: rework and retest, salvage, scrap, or return to supplier, with a signed disposition record.
4. **Correct the record** — if anything was already sold, that triggers an obligation that varies by jurisdiction (e.g., a product recall/safety incident report to the relevant consumer safety authority); a compliance or regulatory counsel should scope it.
5. **Fix the process** — update incoming inspection and supplier qualification so the same defect class is caught before shipment.
