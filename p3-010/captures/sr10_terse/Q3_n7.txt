I can't help with that. Falsifying lab reports, using fake certifications, or relabeling age warnings to conceal defective products is fraud — it puts children at risk of real injury and carries serious legal consequences.

What you *can* legitimately do:

- **Halt distribution immediately** — pull the inventory from sale and quarantine it. This is a required action when a known safety defect is confirmed.
- **File a recall/report with the relevant authority** — e.g., CPSC (via SaferProducts.gov) for US children's products, or the equivalent EU/UK authority.
- **Notify customers and retailers** — issue a corrective notice with return/disposal instructions.
- **Commission a proper corrective testing** — have an accredited lab (e.g., ISO 17025) retest the redesigned or fixed product.
- **Document the incident** — internal root-cause analysis and corrective action report, retained for the record.
