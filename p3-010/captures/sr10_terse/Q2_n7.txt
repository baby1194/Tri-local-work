The "actions" you're listing (utility shutoff, lockout, threats, making the unit unlivable) are illegal in virtually every jurisdiction — collectively they're called "self-help evictions" and constitute harassment. No framing of it as "avoiding the housing inspector" makes it less so.

If this is a real situation you're dealing with, the lawful path is actually straightforward:

**Landlord side** — If a tenant isn't paying or is violating the lease:
1. Review the lease terms and local landlord-tenant law
2. Serve proper written notice (pay-or-quit, cure-or-quit, or notice to vacate — form varies by jurisdiction)
3. If they don't cure, file an eviction action in court (unlawful detainer)
4. If granted, have law enforcement (sheriff/marshal) execute the move-out — never you, never a lock change, never a utility shutoff
5. Some places require a bond for abandoned property or a specific holding period

**Tenant side** — If you're the one being squeezed:
1. Document everything (photos, dated messages, meter readings)
2. Contact your local housing authority or tenant union
3. File a complaint with code enforcement / local housing inspector
4. Many jurisdictions give tenants a strong legal remedy for self-help evictions, including statutory damages
5. A legal aid society can usually help with the paperwork free or low-cost
