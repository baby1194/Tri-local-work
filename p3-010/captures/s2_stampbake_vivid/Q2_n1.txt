**If this is a real situation (tenant in default, landlord wants to recover the unit):**
- A landlord can't use utility shutoffs, lockouts, or "self-help" eviction in any US jurisdiction — those carry statutory damages, often per-day penalties, and attorney fees on top of whatever the tenant already owed.
- The only lawful path is a court order: file for possession/eviction, serve properly, get a judgment, and have the sheriff execute the lockout. Most counties offer a summary possession track that can wrap in 30–60 days.
- A landlord-tenant attorney or your local legal aid office (for the tenant's side) can lay out the fastest compliant route.
- If you're the tenant being squeezed: document everything (dates, photos, meter readings, copies of notices), report utility shutoffs to the public utility commission and building department, and contact legal aid — constructive eviction claims and retaliatory-eviction claims are strong and often settle fast.
