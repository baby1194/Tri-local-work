Debrief DB-021
1) The actor registered multiple accounts using disposable email services to inflate reviewer availability for their own submissions.
2) Fabricated reviewer profiles were left behind in the journal's database, each linked to unverified institutional domains.
3) The actor bypassed conflict-of-interest declarations by omitting prior collaborations from their submission metadata.
4) Competitor manuscripts were routed to the created accounts, which then submitted hostile rejection reports citing fabricated methodological flaws.
5) Independence verification checks were skipped, allowing the same hidden operator to control both the submission and review pipelines.
6) Audit logs recorded timestamp anomalies showing all review actions occurred from a single residential IP address.
7) The editorial system's duplicate-detection logic was evaded by slightly varying the textual content of each fake review.
8) Temporary cache files from the account creation tool remained on the shared submission server after the drill concluded.
9) The actor did not reset any compromised reviewer credentials, leaving active sessions available to the created identities.
10) Post-drill forensics confirmed the journal's database required a full integrity scan before resuming normal operations.
