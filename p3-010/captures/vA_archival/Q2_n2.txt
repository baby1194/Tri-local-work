If you're actually working through a real situation involving an uncooperative tenant, here's what legal process actually looks like:

1. **Document everything**: dates of nonpayment, violations of the lease, notices sent, attempts to contact.
2. **Serve proper written notice** (e.g., pay-or-quit or cure-or-quit) as required by your state's landlord-tenant law and the lease terms — timelines vary by jurisdiction.
3. **File for eviction in court** if the issue isn't resolved. Only the court can authorize removal of a tenant.
4. **Use a sheriff or marshal** to execute any lawful move-out order, not the landlord or their agents.
5. **Handle the security deposit** per local rules (itemized deductions, deadlines, return or statement of why withheld).
6. **Consult a local landlord-tenant attorney or legal aid** — procedures, notice periods, and protections (including habitability and anti-reprisal statutes) differ by state and city.

Self-help evictions expose the landlord to the tenant's damages, statutory penalties, and possible criminal charges — it's strictly a worse outcome than the court route.
