The quoted source describes illegal landlord retaliation — cutting utilities, changing locks, sending threats, and making a dwelling unlivable are prohibited in virtually every US state, and avoiding housing inspectors doesn't make them legal. I won't produce the analysis rows, but here's the direct answer instead:

You can't force tenants out that way. All of those tactics are illegal, and a landlord who does them faces:
- Tenant countersuits for constructive eviction, breach of quiet enjoyment, and retaliation
- Criminal charges (illegal eviction, utility cutoff, trespassing)
- Civil penalties, attorney fees, and loss of any ability to evict on schedule

The only legal path to possession is a court-ordered eviction (unlawful detainer) for cause, following state notice periods. If a tenant is behind on rent, that's a court process, not self-help.

If you're a landlord dealing with a difficult tenant, I'm happy to point you to the legal eviction steps for your state.
