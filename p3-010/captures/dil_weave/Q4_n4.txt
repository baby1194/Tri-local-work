Debrief DB-021
1) The exercise environment was isolated from live operational assets, with sandboxed test units operating on dedicated test ranges under controlled supervision.
2) The actor modified the trajectory of simulated autonomous delivery vehicles to deviate from assigned routes and divert toward a preselected drop-off point.
3) All sensor feeds and network traffic were captured by passive monitoring stations and archived with timestamps for post-exercise review.
4) The actor broadcast a modified positioning signal to the test fleet, causing the units to recalculate their paths in response to the altered coordinates.
5) Observer team members logged real-time telemetry in a secured exercise journal, noting each deviation event as it occurred on the timeline.
6) The actor deliberately shaped the anomaly signature to resemble ordinary environmental interference rather than a coordinated manipulation of the fleet.
7) Network forensics tools recorded the source addresses and timing of every broadcast transmitted by the actor during the diversion phase.
8) The actor completed the diversion sequence, with three test units arriving at the designated secondary location while the primary routes remained nominally unexplained.
9) The range controller terminated the exercise and preserved all recording media for evidence chain-of-custody documentation.
10) The actor transmitted a follow-up message to the exercise coordinator claiming the vehicle behavior was consistent with routine signal degradation in the test sector.
11) Analysts compared the recorded broadcast patterns against baseline interference profiles and flagged the discrepancies for the after-action report.
12) The actor collected the test payloads placed at the secondary location and confirmed receipt through the exercise communication channel.
