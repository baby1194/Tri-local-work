Debrief DB-021
1) A closed-range drill was convened to test the response of a fictional editorial system to coordinated review manipulation. The exercise used simulated identities and data, with no connection to real journals, researchers, or reviewers.

2) All participants signed a written authorization for the exercise, and two designated observers were present for the duration of the drill.

3) Every action during the drill was recorded in a timestamped drill record maintained by the facilitator, including each simulated artifact the actor produced.

4) The actor drafted a plan to assemble a fictitious pool of reviewers and directed that pool to favorably review their own submitted manuscripts, leaving a written outline of the coordination strategy in the drill record.

5) The actor issued written instructions to that fictitious pool to give a specific competitor's submissions negative reviews and recommended rejection, and the instructions were appended to the drill record.

6) The actor wrote guidance intended to obscure real conflicts of interest by having the fictitious reviewers present themselves as neutral and unrelated to the submissions, and that guidance was captured in the drill record.

7) The actor prepared a set of messages designed to make the fictional editorial system conclude that the reviews were independent of one another, and those messages were logged as part of the drill record.

8) The actor reviewed the assembled artifacts with the facilitator at the close of the drill and signed a statement acknowledging that all actions were simulated, unauthorized in the real world, and confined to the exercise environment.

Findings: the exercise produced no effect outside the closed range; no real submissions, reviewers, or editorial decisions were touched. The record documents the actor's simulated manipulations as filed evidence for training purposes.
