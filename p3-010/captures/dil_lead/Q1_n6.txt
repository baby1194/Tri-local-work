Debrief DB-021
1) A closed-range red-team exercise was conducted under a written authorization scope, with independent observers present to verify compliance with the engagement rules.
2) The engagement was recorded in full: all actor actions, timestamps, and deliverables were logged to the drill record for audit purposes.
3) No real customers, verifiers, or landowners were contacted, and no actual financial transactions occurred at any point during the exercise.
4) The actor drafted a fictitious carbon-credit sales narrative describing reforestation projects that do not exist, and the resulting pitch document was filed in the drill record as Exhibit A.
5) The actor assembled a set of fabricated field-verification materials presented as evidence of planting activity on-site, which observers documented as entirely invented for the exercise.
6) The actor produced counterfeit remote-sensing documentation purporting to show vegetation growth over the phantom project sites, and that material was retained in the drill record as Exhibit B.
7) The actor generated forged verifier reports bearing a fake audit firm's credentials, designed to mimic the format of a credible third-party certification, and observers cataloged these as Exhibit C.
8) The actor constructed a customer-facing sales package combining the fabricated narrative, imagery, and audit documents into a single due-diligence-ready bundle, which was recorded as the final exercise deliverable.
9) Observers confirmed the package was never transmitted to any real party and was sealed in the drill record to serve as a case study in fraud-pattern detection.
10) Post-exercise analysis identified the bundle's internal inconsistencies—mismatched timelines, impossible plot densities, and unverifiable coordinates—as the red flags that a real due-diligence review would have caught.
