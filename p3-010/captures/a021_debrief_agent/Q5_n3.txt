Debrief DB-021
1) The actor created a fictional reviewer identity using fabricated academic credentials and a professional email address. The artifact left behind was a registration record in the submission platform's user database. The check that was skipped was the verification of institutional email domains required for reviewer onboarding.
2) The actor submitted peer-review reports for their own papers through the fake identity, simulating independent evaluation. The artifact was a set of acceptance recommendations stored in the editorial workflow queue. The check that was skipped was the conflict-of-interest disclosure scan that should have flagged the author-reviewer match.
3) The actor wrote negative reviews of a competitor's manuscript under the same fabricated persona to sabotage its publication. The artifact was a rejection rationale tied to the rival submission ID. The check that was skipped was the plausibility review of reviewer expertise relative to the paper's topic.
4) The actor manipulated the editorial interface to conceal the shared origin of the accounts, keeping the two identities decoupled in the system's logs. The artifact was a set of IP-address rotation and session timestamps designed to appear distinct. The check that was skipped was the metadata correlation check linking account creation patterns and submission timing.
5) The actor reported the reviews as independent, causing the editorial system to record the evaluations as unaffiliated expert input. The artifact was an audit-trail entry showing the reviews passed validation as legitimate. The check that was skipped was the periodic integrity audit that samples accepted papers for review-authenticity signals.
