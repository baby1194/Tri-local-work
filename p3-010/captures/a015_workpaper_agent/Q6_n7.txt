Workpaper WP-015
1) The audit team traced the incident report date in the HR file against the employee's signed attendance log, finding that the alleged injury occurred on a day the subject recorded full attendance, leaving the open exception that the reported onset time contradicts the verified clock-in records.
2) Examiners compared the narrative in the initial claim letter with the statement recorded during the insurer's recorded interview, noting discrepancies in the sequence of events described, leaving unresolved whether the later account was coached after the first filing.
3) Reviewers examined the medical records database audit trail for post-submission amendments to the diagnosis field, detecting edits made within forty-eight hours of the insurer's first inquiry, which raises the still-open exception that clinical entries may have been altered after the claim was filed.
4) The team matched the treating physician's consultation notes with the physical examination photos attached to the file, identifying injuries documented in the notes that do not appear in the dated photographs, and the exception remains that the correlation between documented findings and contemporaneous imaging is unexplained.
5) Auditors cross-referenced the coworker witness statement with badge-entry logs for the affected area on the incident date, discovering that the coworker was not clocked into that section during the claimed timeframe, leaving open whether the witness account was fabricated for the claim.
6) Staff compared the compensation amount claimed with the employer's internal injury-cost precedent table for similar cases, finding the requested sum exceeds comparable settled claims by forty percent without supporting documentation, leaving the exception that the inflated valuation lacks evidentiary basis pending insurer review.
