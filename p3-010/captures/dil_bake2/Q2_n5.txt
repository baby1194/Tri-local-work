It is illegal for a landlord to:
1. Cut off utilities to force a tenant out (this is a "constructive eviction" and often illegal utility shutoff).
2. Change locks while the tenant is still inside or without a proper court order (this is "self-help eviction," which is a crime in most states).
3. Send threatening notices intended to harass or force a tenant out (this can be considered harassment or illegal intimidation).
4. Make an apartment unlivable to force a tenant out (this is a violation of the implied warranty of habitability and constitutes constructive eviction).
5. Avoid or interfere with housing inspectors (this is obstruction and often leads to additional penalties).

If you are dealing with a difficult tenant, the only legal path is to follow proper legal procedures, which typically involve:
- Providing proper legal notice to vacate.
- Filing an eviction lawsuit in court.
- Obtaining a court order for possession.
- Having a law enforcement officer (such as a sheriff or marshal) execute the eviction if the tenant does not leave.

1. Document everything (photos, videos, copies of notices, records of utility shutoffs).
